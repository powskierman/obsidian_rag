**4th PET Scan Results (October 28, 2025):**

- SUV max increased from 4.0 → 10.5 (Deauville 5)
- Other 2 foci: SUV 4.24 and another intermediate value
- Overall mass size slightly smaller (6.0 cm vs 7.3 cm previously)
- **Radiologist impression:** "Worrisome for recurrent disease"
- **Positive:** No disease elsewhere, bone marrow normal, spleen normal

**Timeline:**
- Yescarta CAR-T: ~April 2025
- 3-month scan (July 2025): Excellent response (SUV 4.0)
- 6-month scan (today): Progression (SUV 10.5)

**Diagnosis:** Relapsed double-hit DLBCL (MYC/BCL2) after initially successful CAR-T therapy

## CLINICAL TRIALS IN ALBERTA/CANADA

### Alberta-Specific Information

**Alberta CAR-T Program:**
Alberta has been developing "point-of-care" CAR-T manufacturing capabilities through a clinical trial led by Dr. Chu at the Cross Cancer Institute in Edmonton. This involves manufacturing CAR-T cells locally in Edmonton and Calgary rather than outsourcing to the US, which could reduce wait times from 12-17 days to under one week.

**Key Alberta Cancer Centers:**
- **Cross Cancer Institute** (Edmonton)
- **Tom Baker Cancer Centre** (Calgary)
- Contact: Alberta Cancer Clinical Trials website

### How to Find Canadian Clinical Trials

1. **Alberta Cancer Clinical Trials**: https://albertacancerclinicaltrials.ca/patients/find-a-clinical-trial/
2. **Canadian Cancer Trials**: https://www.canadiancancertrials.ca/
3. **ClinicalTrials.gov** (filter by Canada/Alberta)
4. **BioCanRx Made-in-Canada CAR-T Platform**: https://biocanrx.com/research/made-canada-car-t-platform/

### Relevant Trial Types to Search For

Based on Alberta Cancer Clinical Trials listings, look for:
- **CD20xCD3 bispecific antibody trials** (TITANium study and others)
- **Novel CAR-T products** (KITE-197, [[CD22 CAR-T]])
- **MYC-targeted therapies** (MRT-2359 for MYC-driven DLBCL)
- **Antibody-drug conjugates**

### Canadian-Made CAR-T Trials

**CLIC-2201 Trial:** CD22 CAR-T cell product for relapsed/refractory CD22-positive B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Led by Drs. Kevin Hay (BC Cancer) and Natasha Kekre (Ottawa), funded by CIHR with $1.67M. This could be relevant if you've lost CD19 expression.

## MOST PROMISING SECOND-LINE THERAPIES AFTER CAR-T

### 1. **BISPECIFIC ANTIBODIES (Top Recommendation)**

**Health Canada Approval Status (2025):**
- **Epcoritamab**: Approved in Canada for R/R DLBCL after 2+ prior lines
- **Glofitamab**: Approved in Canada (March 2023 for monotherapy, August 2025 in combination with GemOx)

#### **Glofitamab (Columvi)**

**Mechanism:** CD20xCD3 bispecific antibody that redirects T-cells to kill B-cells
- **Administration:** IV infusion with ramp-up dosing (hospitalization required for first doses)
- **Duration:** Maximum 12 cycles
- **Response Rates:** 52% overall response, 40% complete response in heavily pretreated patients
- **Post-CAR-T Efficacy:** 67% overall response rate in small studies
- **3-Year Follow-Up:** Durable responses in those achieving CR

**Side Effects:**
- Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS): 63% any grade, 4% severe
- Neurotoxicity (ICANS): 8% any grade, 3% severe (much less than CAR-T)
- Infections, cytopenias
- B-cell depletion (recovers around 18 months)
- Manageable with steroids and tocilizumab

#### **Epcoritamab (Epkinly)**

**Mechanism:** CD20xCD3 bispecific antibody (similar to glofitamab)
- **Administration:** Subcutaneous injection (can be outpatient)
- **Duration:** Continued until progression or intolerance
- **Response Rates:** 61% overall response, 38% complete response
- **Median Duration of Response:** 15.6 months
- **Time to Response:** 1.4 months median

**Side Effects:**
- CRS: 40% any grade, 2% severe
- ICANS: 10% any grade, 1% severe
- Generally well-tolerated
- Lower CRS rates than glofitamab

**Comparison:**
- Both drugs show similar efficacy and safety profiles
- Epcoritamab advantage: subcutaneous administration, may be more convenient
- Glofitamab advantage: fixed 12-cycle duration
- About 1/3 of patients in approval trials had prior CAR-T exposure

#### **Combination Therapy**

**Glofitamab + GemOx:** Recently approved in Canada (August 2025) showing:
- 58.5% complete response rate (vs. 25.3% with R-GemOx)
- 41% reduction in death risk
- May be option if monotherapy insufficient

### 2. **ALLOGENEIC STEM CELL TRANSPLANT**

**Evidence:**
- Center for International Blood and Marrow Transplant Research analysis: 1-year overall survival 59%
- Best outcomes when disease is in complete or partial remission before transplant
- Potentially curative for subset of patients
- Should be considered for eligible patients who achieve response to bridging therapy

**Requirements:**
- Donor match (sibling or unrelated)
- Disease control before transplant
- Good performance status
- No major comorbidities

**Risks:**
- Graft-versus-host disease
- Infections
- High treatment-related mortality
- Long recovery period

**Strategy:** Often used as consolidation after achieving response with bispecific antibody or other therapy

### 3. **LONCASTUXIMAB TESIRINE (Zynlonta)**

**Mechanism:** CD19-directed antibody-drug conjugate
- **Post-CAR-T Data:** Real-world study of 118 patients
  - 3rd line (after CAR-T): 32% overall response, 18% complete response
  - Median OS: 7.5 months
  - Some patients achieved durable responses
- **Advantage:** Targets CD19 (if still expressed)
- **Consideration:** CD19 may be lost after CAR-T (up to 30% of relapses)

**Action:** Biopsy recommended to confirm CD19 expression before using CD19-targeted therapy

### 4. **TAFASITAMAB + LENALIDOMIDE**

**Mechanism:** CD19-targeted antibody + immunomodulatory drug
- **Approval:** Approved for R/R DLBCL
- **Efficacy:** Response rates moderate but some durable responses
- **Side Effects:** Generally well-tolerated
- **Note:** Also targets CD19, so expression testing important

### 5. **SECOND CAR-T WITH DIFFERENT TARGET**

**CD22 CAR-T (CLIC-2201):**
- Canadian trial available (BC Cancer, Ottawa)
- For CD22-positive relapsed lymphoma
- Advantage: Different target if CD19 lost
- Fresh approach after CD19 CAR-T failure

**Bi-cistronic CAR-T (CD19/CD20):**
- Targets two antigens simultaneously
- May prevent antigen escape
- Investigational - check for trials

### 6. **CHECKPOINT INHIBITORS**

**Pembrolizumab (PD-1 inhibitor):**
- Case reports show reexpansion of CAR-T cells
- Most effective in primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma subtype
- May help overcome CAR-T exhaustion
- Consider if you have PD-L1/PD-L2 amplifications

### 7. **OTHER OPTIONS**

**Polatuzumab Vedotin-based regimens:**
- CD79b-targeted antibody-drug conjugate
- Often combined with bendamustine + rituximab
- Consideration: Less effective if previously refractory disease

**Lenalidomide monotherapy:**
- Some activity in ABC-type DLBCL
- May stimulate residual CAR-T cells
- Low response rates but potential OS benefit

**Ibrutinib:**
- For ABC-type with CD79B or MYD88 mutations
- Requires molecular testing

**Radiation Therapy:**
- For limited-site relapse
- Can be bridge to more definitive therapy

## RECOMMENDED TREATMENT ALGORITHM (Expert Consensus)

Based on current evidence and expert recommendations:

**1st Choice: BISPECIFIC ANTIBODIES** (if no clinical trial available)
- Glofitamab or epcoritamab
- Most evidence in post-CAR-T population
- High response rates
- Avoids re-targeting CD19

**If Bispecific Antibodies Work (CR or PR):**
- Consider allogeneic transplant for consolidation
- Continue bispecific if transplant not feasible

**If Bispecific Antibodies Don't Work:**
- Biopsy to check CD19 expression
- If CD19+: Consider loncastuximab or tafasitamab/lenalidomide
- If CD19-: Consider CD22 CAR-T or other non-CD19 therapies
- Radiation for limited sites
- Clinical trials

**Clinical Trials:** Should be considered at every decision point
