# Bispecific Antibodies in Post-CAR-T Failure LBCL: Glofitamab vs Epcoritamab

**Both glofitamab and epcoritamab demonstrate clinically meaningful activity in relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma after CAR-T failure, with complete response rates of 35-40% and durable remissions extending beyond 2-3 years in responders.** While direct head-to-head trials don't exist, pivotal trial data show remarkably similar efficacy: glofitamab achieved 37-40% CR with median duration of CR at 22 months, while epcoritamab achieved 34-36% CR with median duration reaching 36 months in the overall population. Critical limitations include the lack of separately reported progression-free survival data for post-CAR-T subgroups and minimal subgroup analyses examining timing since CAR-T failure or CD19 expression status. Recent 2024-2025 data from ASH and real-world registries confirm these findings translate to clinical practice, though heavily pretreated real-world populations show modestly lower response rates (24-36% CR).

## Pivotal trial efficacy in post-CAR-T populations

### Glofitamab: NP30179 trial results

The NP30179 trial enrolled **52 patients (33.6%) with prior CAR-T therapy** out of 155 total patients with relapsed/refractory LBCL. In the indicated population of 132 patients, 40 (30.3%) had received prior CAR-T.

**Response rates in post-CAR-T patients** demonstrated robust activity with **ORR of 55% (95% CI: 38-71%)** based on FDA analysis of 40 patients. The **complete response rate was 37-40%** across multiple analyses, with the extended follow-up reported at ASH 2023 confirming 37% CR in 52 prior CAR-T patients. This compared favorably to the overall trial population's CR rate of 39-43%, indicating consistent efficacy regardless of prior CAR-T exposure.

**Duration of response** data from the 2023 extended follow-up showed **median duration of CR of 22.0 months (95% CI: 6.7-NE)** in post-CAR-T patients at a median follow-up of 18.3 months for patients achieving CR. Importantly, 65% of complete responses remained ongoing at data cutoff, with an estimated 67% of CR patients remaining in remission at 18 months. The overall population demonstrated median DOR of 18.4 months (95% CI: 11.4-NE).

**Critical limitation:** The pivotal trial did not separately report median progression-free survival specifically for the post-CAR-T subgroup. The overall population showed a 12-month PFS rate of 80% among patients achieving CR at end of treatment.

### Epcoritamab: EPCORE NHL-1 trial results

EPCORE NHL-1 enrolled **61 patients (38.9%) with prior CAR-T therapy** out of 157 total patients with R/R LBCL. Notably, 46 of these 61 patients (75%) were CAR-T refractory, defined as progressive disease within 6 months of CAR-T therapy.

**Response rates in post-CAR-T patients** showed **ORR of 54%** initially reported at ASH 2022, with **complete response rates of 34-36%** (95% CI: 24-49) in the 2-year follow-up analysis published in Leukemia 2024. This compared to 43% CR (95% CI: 33-53) in CAR-T-naive patients, demonstrating preserved activity despite heavy pretreatment.

**Duration of response** data presented a significant gap: **the pivotal trial publications did not separately report median DOR or median PFS specifically for the post-CAR-T subgroup.** The statistical analysis plan specified that subgroup analyses would not be performed on groups with fewer than 20 patients, limiting granular post-CAR-T subset analyses. For the overall population (N=157), median DOR was 17.3 months (95% CI: 9.7-26.5) at 2-year follow-up, with median PFS of 4.4 months (95% CI: 3.0-8.8). Among complete responders specifically, median DOR was not reached, with 64.2% remaining in CR at 24 months.

The **3-year update presented at ASH 2024** (Abstract #198714) showed median duration of CR in the overall population reached **36.1 months (95% CI: 20.2-NR)**, with 63% of complete responders alive at 36 months and remarkably few late progressions beyond 12 months of therapy.

Both agents demonstrate **remarkably similar efficacy** in post-CAR-T populations, with overlapping confidence intervals for CR rates and no statistically significant differences in outcomes between patients with and without prior CAR-T exposure.

## Dedicated post-CAR-T studies and extended analyses

### Glofitamab BICAR study: Purpose-designed post-CAR-T trial

The **BICAR study (NCT04703686)**, published in Nature Cancer in January 2025, represents the only dedicated phase 2 trial specifically enrolling post-CAR-T failure patients. This French LYSA study enrolled **46 patients with R/R DLBCL at first progression after CAR-T therapy**.

**Primary endpoint results** met the prespecified success criteria with **median overall survival of 14.7 months (90% CI: 8.8-NR)** at median follow-up of 15.3 months, significantly exceeding the historical control benchmark of 6.3 months from SCHOLAR-1. 

**Response metrics** demonstrated **best overall metabolic response rate of 76.1%** and **complete metabolic response rate of 45.7%**. However, **median PFS was only 3.8 months (95% CI: 2.4-19.6)**, indicating that while many patients achieved metabolic responses, duration remained limited in the heavily pretreated population.

**Safety profile** was notably favorable with a shortened dosing schedule, achieving **zero grade ≥3 CRS events** and **zero grade ≥3 neurotoxicity**, addressing concerns about sequential T-cell engaging therapy toxicity.

### UK real-world multisite analysis at ASH 2024

Real-world data presented at ASH 2024 (Abstract #203781) from 26 UK centers provided critical insights into timing effects. Among 136 glofitamab patients, **60% (80 patients) had prior CAR-T therapy**.

**Response rates by CAR-T timing** revealed a crucial finding: **complete metabolic response occurred in 28% (95% CI: 16-42%) when glofitamab was administered >3 months post-CAR-T versus only 8% (95% CI: 0.2-36%) when given ≤3 months post-CAR-T** (p=0.16). The overall CMR rate in prior CAR-T patients was 24% (95% CI: 14-35%) compared to 32% in CAR-T-naive patients (p=0.4). Notably, **bendamustine exposure <6 months prior to glofitamab was associated with significantly inferior outcomes** (CRR 9% vs 38%, p=0.01).

## Real-world evidence and registry data

Real-world studies consistently demonstrate **modestly lower efficacy compared to pivotal trials**, reflecting sicker populations with 71-74% of patients considered trial-ineligible due to cytopenias, poor performance status, or other comorbidities.

### Glofitamab real-world outcomes

**Compassionate use programs** from Taiwan (n=34), Turkey (n=43), and Germany showed ORR ranging from 37-67% and CR rates of 21-44%. The Israeli real-world cohort of 35 patients (91% post-CAR-T) showed particularly challenging outcomes with ORR of 34%, CR of 14%, median PFS of 2 months, and median OS of 4 months, though this represented an extremely heavily pretreated population with median 5 prior lines and 97% having ≥4 prior therapies.

The **UK multisite data** remains the most comprehensive real-world dataset, showing overall ORR of 61% with CMR of 27%, though only 24% CR in the post-CAR-T subgroup. Importantly, **6-month duration of CR reached 86%** among responders, indicating that real-world responders achieve similar durability to trial participants.

### Epcoritamab real-world outcomes

Real-world evidence for epcoritamab remains more limited due to later commercial availability. The **UK compassionate access program** (n=38) showed CMR of 26% with **100% of complete responders remaining in remission at 6 months**.

**Japanese case series** published in 2025 demonstrated potential mechanistic insights, with **CAR-T cell copy numbers increasing after epcoritamab administration** without increased exhaustion markers, suggesting bispecific antibodies may enhance residual CAR-T cell activity. A validated case of CD19-negative DLBCL achieving CR after epcoritamab confirmed efficacy independent of CD19 expression.

### Comparative real-world study: 21 US institutions

The largest US real-world cohort presented at ASH 2024 enrolled **245 patients (156 epcoritamab, 89 glofitamab)** across 21 institutions, with 147 (60%) having prior CAR-T therapy.

**Response rates showed remarkable similarity:** epcoritamab ORR 51% with CR 23% versus glofitamab ORR 53% with CR 30%. Median PFS was 2.6 months (95% CI: 2.0-3.8) and median OS 7.8 months (95% CI: 6.2-11.0) for the combined cohort. Critically, **CD20-negative patients had dismal outcomes** with median PFS of only 1.3 months and median OS of 2.0 months, identifying CD20 expression as an essential biomarker.

## Most recent 2024-2025 conference presentations

### ASH 2024 highlights: Combination strategies and long-term follow-up

**Glofitamab plus polatuzumab vedotin** demonstrated substantially improved outcomes in the combination setting. Extended follow-up data (Abstract #194328) with median 23.5 months of follow-up showed that among **28 patients with prior CAR-T (22 refractory to CAR-T), ORR reached 75% with CR rate of 50%**. This represents a marked improvement over monotherapy and suggests combination approaches may overcome resistance mechanisms. Overall population results showed best CR rate of 62%, median PFS of 12.3 months, and median OS of 39.2 months with 64.6% of complete responders remaining in remission at data cutoff.

**EPCORE NHL-1 3-year update** (Abstract #198714) with median follow-up of 37.1 months confirmed durable efficacy with **CR rate of 41%** in the overall population and **median duration of CR of 36.1 months (95% CI: 20.2-NR)**. Among complete responders, median OS was not reached with 63% alive at 36 months. Remarkably, **late progressions were rare:** only 5 of 47 patients progressed between 12-24 months, and only 2 of 29 progressed beyond 24 months, suggesting that patients achieving durable remission beyond 1 year have excellent long-term outcomes.

The **CAR-T naive subanalysis** (Abstract #199225) of 96 patients showed ORR 61% with CR 45%, median duration of CR 28.6 months (95% CI: 15.8-NR), and **46% remaining in CR at 36 months**, demonstrating that epcoritamab can be effectively used before or after CAR-T therapy with preserved efficacy.

### ASCO and EHA 2024: STARGLO phase 3 and optimized dosing

<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">The **STARGLO phase 3 trial** presented at EHA 2024 (LB3438) marked the first randomized controlled trial of a bispecific antibody in LBCL, comparing glofitamab plus GemOx to rituximab plus GemOx in patients with R/R DLBCL ineligible for ASCT. At 24.7 months median follow-up, glofitamab demonstrated **significant survival benefit with median OS not reached versus 13.5 months** (HR 0.60, 95% CI: 0.42-0.85), **median PFS of 13.8 versus 3.6 months** (HR 0.41, 95% CI: 0.29-0.58), and **CR rate of 58.5% versus 25.3%**. </mark>This establishes bispecific antibodies as superior to standard chemotherapy in earlier relapse settings, though the trial did not specifically focus on post-CAR-T patients.^starglo ^5f6df0

## Subgroup analyses: Time, mechanism, and biomarkers

### Time since CAR-T failure

**German/European multicenter data** analyzing 92 patients with R/R LBCL treated with bispecific antibodies after CAR-T failure revealed **timing as the most critical prognostic factor:**

- **Early relapse (≤3 months post-CAR-T):** ORR 29%, median PFS 2.2 months, CR 14%
- **Intermediate relapse (4-6 months):** ORR 54%, median PFS 3.7 months, CR 25%  
- **Late relapse (>6 months):** ORR 60%, median PFS 10.5 months, CR 44%

This finding was corroborated by UK real-world data showing CR rate of 28% when glofitamab was given >3 months post-CAR-T versus 8% when given ≤3 months. The biological mechanism likely relates to immune reconstitution, with T-cell functionality recovering over time after CAR-T-related lymphodepletion.

### Reason for CAR-T failure: Progressive disease versus relapse

**Granular data distinguishing mechanism of CAR-T failure remains extremely limited.** EPCORE NHL-1 defined CAR-T refractory as progression within 6 months (n=46, 75% of prior CAR-T patients) but did not publish separate efficacy outcomes for this subgroup beyond noting that the overall post-CAR-T CR rate was 34%. A small subset analysis showed patients **not refractory to prior CAR-T (n=15) achieved ORR of 80.0% (95% CI: 51.9-95.7) and CR of 53.0% (95% CI: 26.6-78.7)**, though formal statistical analysis was not performed due to small sample size.

NP30179 noted that 83% of enrolled patients were refractory to their most recent therapy but did not separately analyze outcomes by type of CAR-T failure.

### CD19 expression status

**Neither pivotal trial reported detailed subgroup analyses by CD19 expression status in post-CAR-T patients.** This represents a critical knowledge gap given that 20-30% of CAR-T failures involve CD19-negative relapse.

Mechanistically, both glofitamab and epcoritamab target CD20 rather than CD19, theoretically making them effective regardless of CD19 status and potentially addressing a key CAR-T resistance mechanism. The Japanese case report of CD19-negative DLBCL achieving CR with epcoritamab provides proof-of-concept evidence, and glofitamab's CD20 targeting is explicitly noted as addressing CD19-negative relapses in mechanism-of-action discussions. However, **systematic analysis of CD19 expression as a predictive biomarker has not been published.**

Real-world data clearly establish **CD20 expression as essential:** the US multicenter study demonstrated that CD20-negative patients had median PFS of only 1.3 months and median OS of 2.0 months with either bispecific antibody.

### Prior lines of therapy

The **overall populations** in both trials had median 3 prior lines of therapy (range 2-11), with 59% of NP30179 patients and 52% of EPCORE NHL-1 patients having ≥3 prior lines. Subgroup analyses showed **generally consistent CR rates across different numbers of prior lines** in the overall populations.

However, **specific breakdown by number of prior lines within the post-CAR-T subgroup was not published** for either trial. Real-world studies suggest heavily pretreated populations (≥4-5 prior lines) show inferior outcomes, with the Israeli study (median 5 prior lines, 97% with ≥4 lines) demonstrating particularly poor results (ORR 34%, median OS 4 months).

### Additional prognostic factors: MRD and tumor burden

**Minimal residual disease (MRD) analysis** in EPCORE NHL-1 revealed **MRD negativity as a powerful predictor of durability:** 45.4% of evaluable patients (54/119) achieved MRD negativity. At the Cycle 3 Day 1 landmark, 36-month PFS/OS rates were 52%/55% in MRD-negative patients versus 18%/30% in MRD-positive patients. By Cycle 13, 98% of MRD-evaluable patients were MRD-negative, suggesting that continued treatment may deepen responses.

**Baseline total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV)** analysis from NP30179 showed higher TMTV associated with increased CRS risk and worse outcomes: 12-month PFS was 50.1% for TMTV below median versus 16.8% for TMTV above median (HR 2.6, 95% CI: 1.7-3.9, p<0.0001).

## Head-to-head comparisons and indirect evidence

### No direct randomized trials exist

**No head-to-head randomized controlled trials comparing glofitamab and epcoritamab have been conducted or are currently planned.** All comparative evidence derives from indirect treatment comparisons, which carry substantial methodological limitations.

### Matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC)

The **most rigorous indirect comparison** (Allan et al., Blood 2024, ASH Abstract #2349) compared EPCORE NHL-1 (N=157) to NP30179 (N=155) using unanchored MAIC methodology adjusting for baseline characteristic imbalances.

**Overall survival analysis** revealed a biphasic pattern with different treatment effects over time, requiring piece-wise analysis due to violation of proportional hazards. **From Cycle 1 to Month 6, outcomes were comparable:** HR 1.08 (95% CI: 0.71-1.64, p=0.72). However, **beyond 6 months, epcoritamab showed a significant survival advantage:** HR 0.60 (95% CI: 0.37-0.98, p=0.04). When the analysis started at Cycle 3 with longer follow-up, the HR was 0.68 (95% CI: 0.46-1.01, p=0.05), consistently favoring epcoritamab.

**Progression-free survival** showed similar patterns: initial analysis demonstrated comparable PFS (HR 1.06, 95% CI: 0.79-1.41, p=0.71), but with longer follow-up starting at Cycle 3, PFS after Month 6 favored epcoritamab (HR 0.56, 95% CI: 0.33-0.97, p=0.04).

**Duration of complete response** appeared longer for epcoritamab (36.1 months) compared to glofitamab (26.9 months), potentially explaining the late OS divergence. Median OS was 18.5 months for epcoritamab versus 11.5 months for glofitamab in the MAIC analysis.

### Cost-effectiveness with indirect comparison

A **separate MAIC-based cost-effectiveness analysis** (Fox et al., Blood 2024, ASH Abstract #7614) from the US payer perspective confirmed the OS benefit, with piece-wise OS hazard ratios of 1.18 before 6 months (non-significant) but **0.57 after 6 months (95% CI: 0.34-0.94)** favoring epcoritamab. This translated to **2.33 additional life years and 1.32 additional QALYs**, yielding an **ICER of $88,189 per QALY**, considered cost-effective at the $150,000/QALY threshold despite higher total treatment costs.

**Conflicting cost analysis** (Mahmoudjafari et al., Blood 2023) found glofitamab resulted in cost savings of $68,195 at 1 year and $503,075 lifetime due to its fixed 12-cycle duration versus treat-to-progression approach, highlighting how different modeling assumptions yield divergent conclusions.

### Health technology assessment conclusions

Both **NICE (UK)** and **CADTH (Canada)** have recommended both drugs for R/R DLBCL after ≥2 prior lines. NICE explicitly noted the drugs have similar mechanisms and both received positive recommendations (glofitamab TA927, epcoritamab TA954 in 2024). CADTH reviews highlighted **comparable efficacy profiles** from single-arm trials with post-CAR-T CR rates of 35% (glofitamab) versus 34% (epcoritamab).

**Health Canada approved both drugs** but with more restrictive indications limited to patients who "previously received or are unable to receive CAR-T therapy," narrower than FDA, EMA, and MHRA approvals.

### Practical differences influencing choice

| Feature | Glofitamab | Epcoritamab |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Administration** | Intravenous | Subcutaneous |
| **Pretreatment** | Obinutuzumab required | None |
| **Treatment duration** | Fixed 12 cycles | Until progression |
| **Dosing schedule** | Every 21 days | Weekly→biweekly→monthly |
| **Median OS (MAIC)** | 11.5 months | 18.5 months |
| **Healthcare utilization** | 90.6 chair hours/6 months | 55.8 chair hours/6 months |

Subcutaneous epcoritamab reduced healthcare provider time by 29% and chair time by 38% compared to intravenous glofitamab in time-motion studies, with capacity implications for high-volume centers.

## Safety profile and sequencing considerations

### Cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity

Both agents demonstrate **manageable CRS predominantly in early cycles.** Glofitamab showed CRS rates of 40-70% (mostly grade 1-2, with grade 3 in 2-4% and rare grade 4-5 events). Epcoritamab showed CRS in 37-60% (grade 3 in 4%, no grade 4-5 in most series). **ICANS occurred in 4-7% with both agents**, substantially lower than CAR-T therapy's 8-11% rate of grade ≥3 neurotoxicity.

The **BICAR study's shortened dosing schedule** achieved zero grade ≥3 CRS or neurotoxicity events, demonstrating that optimization strategies can further reduce toxicity. Real-world UK data confirmed no treatment-related deaths with glofitamab in 142 patients and no treatment-related deaths with epcoritamab in 38 patients when managed with appropriate protocols. ^5c4595

### Clinical practice implications for sequencing

**Bendamustine exposure** <6 months prior to glofitamab associated with dramatically inferior outcomes (CRR 9% vs 38%, p=0.01), suggesting this chemotherapy impairs T-cell function necessary for bispecific antibody activity.

**Timing after CAR-T failure** emerges as the most actionable prognostic factor: delaying bispecific antibody initiation >3 months post-CAR-T improves CR rates nearly 4-fold (28% vs 8%) compared to immediate salvage. However, this must be balanced against disease progression risk during observation.

**CD20 expression** should be confirmed before initiating therapy, as CD20-negative patients have minimal benefit (median PFS 1.3 months, median OS 2.0 months).

## Evidence gaps and future directions

### Critical missing data

Neither pivotal trial reported **median progression-free survival specifically for post-CAR-T subgroups**, representing the single most important data gap. The trials were not designed or powered for formal statistical comparisons between post-CAR-T and CAR-T-naive subgroups.

**Time since CAR-T failure stratification** beyond broad categories (≤3 months vs >3 months) has not been systematically analyzed. **Mechanism of CAR-T failure** (primary refractory vs relapsed disease, CD19-negative vs CD19-positive relapse) lacks detailed correlation with bispecific antibody outcomes.

**CD19 expression status analysis** in post-CAR-T patients remains unpublished despite biological rationale suggesting CD20-targeted bispecifics address CD19-negative escape.

### Emerging insights and mechanistic questions

**CAR-T cell reactivation** data from Japanese series suggests epcoritamab may enhance residual CAR-T cell activity, with increased CAR copy numbers and cytotoxicity markers without increased exhaustion markers. Whether this represents a class effect or drug-specific phenomenon requires investigation.

**MRD-guided therapy** may optimize treatment duration, as 98% of EPCORE NHL-1 patients evaluable at Cycle 13 were MRD-negative, yet some remained on treatment substantially longer. Whether fixed-duration approaches guided by MRD negativity could match continuous therapy outcomes remains unresolved.

**Combination strategies** show promise, with glofitamab plus polatuzumab demonstrating 50% CR in post-CAR-T patients compared to 24-40% with monotherapy. Whether combining bispecifics with other novel agents (BTK inhibitors, immunomodulators, or even CAR-T cells) could further improve outcomes warrants exploration.

## Clinical synthesis and recommendations

Both glofitamab and epcoritamab represent **effective options for post-CAR-T failure LBCL** with comparable efficacy (CR rates 34-40%) and durable responses in one-third of patients. Treatment selection should be individualized based on administration route preference, treatment duration philosophy (fixed 12 cycles vs continuous), and practical factors including IV access and travel burden.

**For post-CAR-T patients specifically,** timing matters: delaying treatment >3 months post-CAR-T improves outcomes if disease kinetics permit observation. Avoiding recent bendamustine exposure and confirming CD20 expression optimize response probability. MRD assessment may identify patients achieving deep remissions who could discontinue therapy even with continuous-dosing agents.

**The indirect comparison suggesting OS benefit for epcoritamab** must be interpreted cautiously given methodological limitations of unanchored MAIC, different trial populations, and follow-up duration disparities. Both drugs have secured regulatory approval and positive health technology assessments based on single-arm trial data showing consistent benefit in heavily pretreated populations. A prospective randomized trial would definitively establish comparative efficacy but is unlikely given both drugs' established roles in clinical practice.

**Real-world evidence confirms** trial efficacy translates to community practice, though with modestly lower response rates reflecting sicker, trial-ineligible populations. The achievement of median OS of 8-15 months in post-CAR-T patients represents meaningful improvement over historical controls (SCHOLAR-1: 6.3 months) and provides a bridge to subsequent therapies or, in responding patients, potential cure with durable remissions exceeding 3 years.
