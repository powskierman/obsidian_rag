# Highlights

Small parts should be designed with a single or double layer 'brim' around the base of the part, which can easily be trimmed off after removal from the bed. ([517](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=517)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462567

---

Stress relieving structures such as holes or slots in long continuous parts will help prevent stresses from building up at the edges of the print. ([528](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=528)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462568

---

To ensure void free construction, the width of thin vertical (Z-axis) design features should not be less than 2 nozzle widths wherever possible, and should not exceed 2x the number of specified perimeters (for example 4 nozzle thicknesses, if 2 perimeters are to be specified in the slicer). If they need to be stronger, they should be made significantly wider, so that they will be wide enough to accommodate proper infilling (see Chapter VII, Understanding the slicer).   Use uniform wall thicknesses where possible and a generous radius at corners to minimize stress localization. The inside corner radius should be at least equal to the wall thickness when possible. ([692](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=692)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462569

---

Where structural integrity is important, wall thickness transitions should be tapered and be at least three times as long as the transition difference, more if possible. For example, a 1 millimeter wall thickness change should ideally take place over about of 3 millimeters or more, unless the part has a continuous taper. ([701](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=701)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462570

---

Where voids or hollows are desired in a piece, the internal void area must be connected or 'vented' either directly or indirectly through the outside wall of the piece, as most slicing software will ignore “orphaned” voids and fill them with infill. ([862](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=862)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462571

---

Screws may be threaded into a hole 0.8 times their nominal diameter, so roughly 0.8 times (D+.5mm) when encroachment is added ([886](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=886)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462572

---

For glued or friction fits, joinery pieces should usually be given a dimensional gap of 0.5 to 0.75 millimeters total between the interlocking parts. ([888](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=888)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462573

---

Generally, corners should be rounded where possible so that the inner radius of the corner is equal or greater to the wall thickness of the part. ([905](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=905)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462574

---

For non-structural purposes, a top and bottom layer thickness of 2 layers or at least 0.3 millimeters is usually sufficient. ([943](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=943)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462575

---

Where solid walls or shells are desired, the number of perimeters times 2 (for inner and outer surface) multiplied by the nozzle thickness should correspond to the wall thickness of the structure to ensure solidity and reduce the possibility of a poorly bonded structure. ([951](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0107KLKVK&location=951)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334462576
