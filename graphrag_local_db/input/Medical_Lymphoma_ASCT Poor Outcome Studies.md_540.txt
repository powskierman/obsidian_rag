### Notes
- <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Several studies have identified factors associated with inferior outcomes after #ASCT for DLBCL, including:</mark>
	- Poor response to pre-transplant therapy
	- <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Advanced age (>60 years)</mark>
	- High International Prognostic Index (IPI) score (≥3)
	- <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Extraneousodal involvement (e.g., bone marrow</mark>, CNS)
	- <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Co-expression of [[MYC]] and [[BCL2]] in ≥50% of tumor cells</mark>
- Below are the studies corresponding to the factors:
1. Poor Response to Pre-Transplant Therapy
	•	The SCHOLAR-1 study demonstrated that <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">patients with refractory DLBCL, defined as no response to their last chemotherapy or relapse within 12 months post-#ASCT, had poor outcomes</mark>, with a median overall survival (OS) of only 6.3 months.
	•	A study by Sauter et al. highlighted that patients with FDG-avid disease pre-#ASCT (Deauville score of 4) had suboptimal outcomes, although some could still achieve long-term survival.
2. Advanced Age (>60 Years)
	•	A study using the Center for International Blood and Marrow Transplant Research database compared patients aged 60–69 years versus those ≥70 years. While #ASCT was feasible in older patients, <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">those ≥70 years had higher mortality</mark> and worse post-relapse OS compared to younger cohorts.
	•	Another retrospective analysis identified age >60 years as an independent prognostic factor for inferior survival in DLBCL patients undergoing #ASCT.
3. High International Prognostic Index (IPI) Score (≥3)
	•	The prognostic impact of high IPI scores was underscored in a study that found upfront #ASCT improved outcomes in high-risk DLBCL patients who achieved complete remission after R-CHOP therapy. However, those with IPI scores ≥3 still had worse outcomes compared to lower-risk groups.
	•	The REFINE study also linked intermediate-high or high IPI scores at the time of primary treatment failure with significantly inferior survival rates post-#ASCT.
4. Extranodal Involvement (e.g., Bone Marrow, CNS)
	•	A population-based study using SEER data found that extranodal involvement in sites such as the gastrointestinal tract, liver/pancreas, and pulmonary regions was associated with worse OS compared to nodal DLBCL.
	•	Additional studies have shown that bone marrow and CNS involvement are adverse prognostic factors in DLBCL, contributing to poor outcomes post-#ASCT.
5. Co-Expression of MYC and BCL2 in ≥50% of Tumor Cells
	•	A study by Johnson et al. demonstrated that MYC/BCL2 co-expression (double-expressor phenotype) is associated with an aggressive clinical course and inferior survival, with 5-year OS and progression-free survival (PFS) rates below 30%.
	•	Another multicenter retrospective review confirmed that double-hit lymphomas (MYC and BCL2 rearrangements) and double-expressor lymphomas were linked to poor PFS and OS after #ASCT.
These studies collectively highlight the multifactorial nature of poor outcomes following #ASCT in DLBCL, emphasizing the need for tailored treatment strategies for high-risk patients.
