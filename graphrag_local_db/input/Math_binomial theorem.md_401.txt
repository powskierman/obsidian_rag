- The binomial theorem (or binomial expansion) is a result of expanding the powers of binomials or sums of two terms. The coefficients of the terms in the expansion are the *binomial coefficients*.
	- $(x+y)^1$ = $x+y$
	- $(x+y)^2$ = $x^2+2xy+y^2$
	- $(x+y)^3$ = $x^3+3xy^2+3x^2y+y^3$
	- $(x+y)^4$ =	$x^4+4xy^3+6x^2y^2+4x^3y+y^4$
## Theorem
Let n be a positive integer, and x and y real  (or complex , or polynomials). The coefficient of $x^k y^{n−k}$, in the k$\text{th}$ term in the expansion of $(x+y)^n$, is equal to $(n^k)(k^n)$, where:
$${\left(\begin{array}{l}n \\ k\end{array}\right)=\frac{n !}{(n-k) ! k !}}$$
so:
$$
(x+y)^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\left(\begin{array}{l}
n \\
r
\end{array}\right) x^{n-r} y^{r}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\left(\begin{array}{l}
n \\
r
\end{array}\right) x^{r} y^{n-r} .
$$
or:
The **binomial theorem** states that for any positive integer nn, we have:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+y)^{n} &=\left(\begin{array}{l}
n \\
0
\end{array}\right) x^{n}+\left(\begin{array}{l}
n \\
1
\end{array}\right) x^{n-1} y+\cdots+\left(\begin{array}{c}
n \\
n-1
\end{array}\right) x y^{n-1}+\left(\begin{array}{l}
n \\
n
\end{array}\right) y^{n} \\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\begin{array}{l}
n \\
k
\end{array}\right) x^{n-k} y^{k}
\end{aligned}
$$
