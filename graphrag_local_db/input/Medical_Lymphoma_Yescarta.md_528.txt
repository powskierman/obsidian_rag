### Notes
-  Double-/Triple-Hit Lymphomas: ==Yescarta is specifically indicated for high-grade B-cell lymphomas with MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6 rearrangements (double-/triple-hit). In the ZUMA-12 trial, patients with these rearrangements achieved an 86% complete response rate and 81% estimated 3-year overall survival when treated with Yescarta as first-line therapy.==
- Yescarta (Axi-cel) (axicabtagene ciloleucel) is available in Alberta. It is reimbursed and offered as a treatment option for adult patients with relapsed or refractory large B-cell lymphoma after two or more lines of systemic therapy. The therapy is administered at specialized centers in Alberta, including the Tom Baker Cancer Centre in Calgary and the Cross Cancer Institute in Edmonton, which are certified to provide CAR T-cell therapies like Yescarta
- Yescarta (axicabtagene ciloleucel) is a CAR T-cell therapy developed by Kite Pharma, a Gilead company. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">It is a form of personalized immunotherapy that uses a patient’s own genetically modified T cells to treat specific types of aggressive blood cancers</mark>, particularly non-Hodgkin lymphomas such as large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL) and follicular lymphoma (FL). Below is an overview of its mechanism, indications, administration, efficacy, and safety profile.
- Mechanism of Action
- Yescarta is a CD19-directed CAR T-cell therapy. It involves:
	1.	Leukapheresis: Extracting the patient’s T cells (a type of white blood cell).
	2.	Genetic Modification: Engineering the T cells in a laboratory to express a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) that targets the CD19 protein found on B cells, including cancerous ones.
	3.	Infusion: Reintroducing the modified T cells into the patient’s bloodstream. These CAR T cells identify and attack cancer cells expressing CD19, leading to their destruction.
- #### Indications
- <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Yescarta is approved for treating adults with:
	•	Large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL) that is refractory to first-line chemoimmunotherapy </mark>or relapses within 12 months after treatment.
	•	Relapsed or refractory LBCL after two or more lines of systemic therapy, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma (PMBCL), high-grade B-cell lymphoma, and DLBCL arising from follicular lymphoma.
	•	Relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma (FL) after three or more lines of systemic therapy.
	It is not indicated for patients with primary central nervous system lymphoma.
- #### Administration
- <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Yescarta is administered as a single intravenous infusion following preparatory chemotherapy to deplete existing white blood cells and enhance the effectiveness of the CAR T cells. The infusion typically takes less than 30 minutes, but patients must remain under close observation for at least 7 days at a certified healthcare facility and stay near the facility for up to 4 weeks due to potential side effects</mark>.
- #### Efficacy
- Clinical trials have demonstrated Yescarta’s effectiveness:
	•	<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">In the ZUMA-7 trial for LBCL, it significantly improved event-free survival compared to standard treatments, with some patients achieving durable remissions. At 4 years, overall survival was 54.6% </mark>for Yescarta-treated patients versus 46% for those receiving standard care.
	•	In relapsed/refractory FL, Yescarta has shown high response rates, offering a new option for patients who have exhausted other therapies.
- #### Safety Profile
- Yescarta carries significant risks and requires careful monitoring:
- Common Side Effects
	•	Fatigue
	•	Fever
	•	Nausea
	•	Low blood pressure
	•	Diarrhea
	•	Headache
	•	Muscle pain.
- <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Serious Risks
	1.	Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS): A potentially life-threatening inflammatory response causing fever, low blood pressure, respiratory distress, and multi-organ failure. CRS typically occurs within days of infusion but can be managed with medications like tocilizumab.
	2.	Neurological Toxicities: Symptoms include confusion, memory loss, seizures, or encephalopathy. These can occur alongside or after CRS.
	3.	Increased risk of infections and secondary cancers due to immune system suppression.
	4.	Long-term side effects include hypogammaglobulinemia (low antibody levels) and potential secondary malignancies.</mark>
- #### Monitoring Requirements
- Yescarta can only be administered at certified centers equipped to manage severe side effects. Patients are closely monitored post-infusion for at least 10 days and must remain near the treatment center for several weeks.
- #### Conclusion
- Yescarta represents a groundbreaking advance in cancer treatment by harnessing the immune system to target aggressive lymphomas that have resisted conventional therapies. While it offers hope for long-term remission in many patients, its use requires specialized care due to serious risks like CRS and neurological toxicities.
