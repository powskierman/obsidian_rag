### Notes
- Epcoritamab is a novel humanized IgG1-bispecific T-cell engaging antibody that works through a unique dual-targeting mechanism. It simultaneously binds to CD20 on B cells and CD3 on T cells, creating a bridge between these cells[4][9]. This dual binding induces T-cell activation, triggers the release of proinflammatory cytokines, and ultimately results in the selective lysis of CD20-expressing B cells, including malignant B cells[4][9].
### Mechanism of Action
Epcoritamab (brand name Epkinly™) functions through:
- **Bispecific binding**: It targets both CD20 (on B cells) and CD3 (on T cells)[4][9]
- **T-cell redirection**: By bringing T cells into proximity with malignant B cells, it promotes T-cell-mediated cytotoxic activity[9]
- **Cytokine release**: The engagement triggers release of proinflammatory cytokines[4]
- **Selective cell death**: This process results in lysis of CD20+ malignant B cells[4][9]
The drug is administered subcutaneously, which is different from many other cancer therapies that require intravenous administration[1].
### Clinical Indications
Epcoritamab is approved for treatment of several B-cell malignancies:
- Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL)
- High-grade B-cell lymphoma (HGBCL)
- Primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma (PMBCL)
- Follicular lymphoma[4][7]
It's particularly valuable for patients with relapsed or refractory disease who have failed previous lines of therapy[6][7].
## Side Effects
### Common Side Effects
The most frequently reported side effects include:
- Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) - occurs in approximately 50% of patients, primarily during Cycle 1[2][4]
- Fatigue[2][5]
- Musculoskeletal pain (muscle, joint, or bone pain)[2][5]
- Injection site reactions (rash, swelling, itching)[2][5]
- Pyrexia (fever)[2][5]
- Gastrointestinal issues (nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea)[2][4][5]
- Headache[2]
### Serious Side Effects
More serious adverse effects that require close monitoring include:
- **Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS)**: Can be serious or life-threatening, though most cases are Grade 1. Symptoms include fever, hypotension, hypoxia, chills, and tachycardia[2][4]
- **Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS)**: Can be life-threatening in rare cases[2][4]
- **Infections**: Including severe infections[2]
- **Myelosuppression**: Leading to neutropenia, anemia, or thrombocytopenia[4]
- **Pleural effusion**: Reported in about 9% of patients[4]
- **Tumor lysis syndrome**: Occurs in approximately 1% of patients[4]
## Administration and Dosing
- To mitigate the risk of CRS, epcoritamab is administered using a step-up dosing schedule, with gradual dose increases[4][10]. The drug reaches its maximum concentration (Tmax) about 4 days after the first full dose in Cycle 1, and has a half-life of 22-25 days for the full 48 mg dose[4][10].
- Prophylactic medications are recommended before administration to reduce the risk of CRS[4]. Patients should be monitored closely for 24 hours following the first full dose administration[4].
- Most adverse events, particularly CRS, occur during the first 12 weeks of treatment (cycles 1-3), with the incidence declining thereafter[1][4]. The majority of CRS events (61%) occur after the first full 48mg dose on Cycle 1, Day 15[4].
Epcoritamab represents an important advancement in targeted immunotherapy for B-cell malignancies, offering a novel mechanism of action and potential benefit for patients with limited treatment options.
