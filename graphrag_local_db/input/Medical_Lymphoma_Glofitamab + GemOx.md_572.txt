### Notes
- Glofitamab in combination with GemOx (gemcitabine and oxaliplatin) shows significantly improved efficacy for relapsed/refractory high-grade B-cell lymphoma (HGBCL), including some double-hit cases, compared to standard R-GemOx chemotherapy alone. Results from phase III trials and real-world data reveal higher complete response rates and longer progression-free survival with Glofitamab + GemOx, though data specific to the double-hit subset remains limited and requires further study.
#### Clinical Trial Results and Efficacy
- The STARGLO phase III trial demonstrated a clear benefit for Glofitamab + GemOx in transplant-ineligible patients with relapsed/refractory DLBCL and HGBCL, with a complete response rate of 58.5% versus 25.3% for R-GemOx, and a 41% reduction in risk of death (hazard ratio 0.59, p=0.011). Median overall survival was extended to 25.5 months compared to 12.9 months with standard therapy, and progression-free survival was significantly longer . GemOx itself provides immunomodulatory effects, further enhancing response to bispecific antibodies like Glofitamab .
- Double-Hit Lymphoma Subset
- For “double-hit” HGBCL (with MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6 rearrangements), case reports and small retrospective studies suggest Glofitamab’s activity, even in chemoresistant contexts. One published case of HGBCL-MYC/BCL2 showed durable partial response after multiple cycles of Glofitamab, sometimes augmented with lenalidomide. However, phase III trials such as STARGLO excluded double-hit and triple-hit patients, so current evidence for this subgroup comes primarily from off-protocol and retrospective data, which are promising but not conclusive .
- Safety Profile
- Glofitamab + GemOx is generally well tolerated, but serious infection rates are higher (26%) than with standard R-GemOx (13%). Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and neurotoxicity remain important considerations for bispecific antibody therapy in high-risk populations, and long-term safety data are still emerging .
#### Limitations and Future Directions
•	Double-hit HGBCL evidence is still limited to small reports; larger studies are needed to confirm efficacy and safety in this molecularly defined subgroup.
•	Multicenter trials with biomarker-driven eligibility and adaptive design are recommended to address diagnostic complexity and recruitment challenges.
•	Ongoing research is focusing on combining Glofitamab with additional agents (e.g. lenalidomide, polatuzumab vedotin) and on molecular stratification for personalized approaches .
#### In conclusion 
Combining Glofitamab with GemOx offers marked clinical benefit for high-risk lymphoma, but for double-hit HGBCL, data is encouraging though still preliminary, and further targeted clinical trials are needed.
