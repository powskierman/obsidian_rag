Neapolitan-style pizza? Use brine-packed fior di latte or mozzarella di bufala. — location: [1379](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B012KJYR3O&location=1379) ^ref-44644

Two main types of mozzarella are used in Italy: fresh cheese made from cow’s milk, called fior di latte, and fresh cheese made from water buffalo milk, called mozzarella di bufala. Bufala is both richer in fat and more acidic (a perfect complement!) than fior di latte; — location: [1380](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B012KJYR3O&location=1380) ^ref-20270

To prevent over-liquefaction in the home oven when using brine-packed fior di latte, add the cheese about 4 minutes into the baking cycle. — location: [1402](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B012KJYR3O&location=1402) ^ref-65103

For delicately flavored fresh cheeses like burrata, the magic mozzarella that’s filled with whole cream, put it on top of the pizza right after it comes out of the oven. — location: [1403](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B012KJYR3O&location=1403) ^ref-32406

The yellower the mozzarella, the more age and saltiness it is likely to have. Try different kinds of low-moisture mozzarella to find the one you like best. As a rule, I prefer whiter, fresh brands like Calabro and Losurdo for American pizzas. If you’re using low-moisture mozzarella, shred your own cheese. It takes just a minute with a decent box grater. Grate over the large holes. — location: [1443](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B012KJYR3O&location=1443) ^ref-52141
