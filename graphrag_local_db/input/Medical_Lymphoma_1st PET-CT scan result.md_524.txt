### What this means:
- Key Findings
	1.	Retroperitoneal Lymph Node Mass:
	![[Pasted image 20241223142746 1.png]]
	•	A large mass in the retroperitoneal region (the area behind the abdominal cavity) was identified. It is described as intensely FDG avid, meaning it shows a high uptake of the radioactive tracer (FDG), which is common in cancerous or highly active cells.
	•	The mass measures 6.2 x 10.0 cm axially (width and depth) and 9.1 cm cranio-caudally (height).
	•	The maximum standardized uptake value (SUV max) is 28.9, which is significantly elevated, indicating high metabolic activity typically seen in lymphoma.
	2.	Marrow Lesion in the Right Humerus:
	•	A small but intensely FDG avid lesion was found in the bone marrow of your distal right humerus (upper arm bone). This lesion has an SUV max of 11.0, also suggesting lymphomatous involvement.
	•	The lesion measures approximately 1 cm in width and extends over a length of 2 cm.
	3.	Spleen, Other Lymph Nodes, and Organs:
	•	The spleen appears normal in size and metabolic activity, with no signs of disease.
	•	No other hypermetabolic lymph nodes were detected above the diaphragm (in areas like the chest or neck).
	•	Other major organs, including the liver, pancreas, adrenal glands, kidneys, and lungs, show no abnormal FDG uptake or suspicious findings.
	•	There are minor findings such as mild diverticulosis (a benign condition affecting the colon) and bilateral renal cysts (non-cancerous fluid-filled sacs on the kidneys).
	4.	Prostate:
	•	The prostate is described as “bulky,” which may suggest enlargement but does not indicate FDG avidity or malignancy based on this scan.
Impression
	•	The findings strongly suggest lymphoma involving:
	1.	A large retroperitoneal lymph node mass.
	2.	A secondary site in the bone marrow of the right humerus.
	•	No other significant areas of disease were detected elsewhere in the body.
What Does This Mean?
	•	The PET/CT scan supports a diagnosis of lymphoma, consistent with my biopsy results.
	•	The retroperitoneal mass is likely the primary site of involvement.
	•	The lesion in your right humerus indicates that lymphoma has spread to at least one additional site (bone marrow involvement).
	•	The absence of other FDG avid abnormalities suggests that the disease has not widely spread to other organs or lymph nodes above the diaphragm.
Next Steps
	•	Your healthcare team will likely use this information to stage your lymphoma and plan treatment. Staging considers factors such as:
	•	The size and location of masses.
	•	Whether multiple sites are involved.
	•	Whether disease is confined to lymph nodes or has spread to other organs/bone marrow.
	•	Treatment typically includes chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or radiation, depending on the type and stage of lymphoma.
