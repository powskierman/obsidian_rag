- ## How to Solve by Substitution
	- Substitution in when we replace one variable with another. 

To solve a [[system of equations]] by substitution, follow the steps outlined below: 

1. Isolate a variable in one of the equations (usually easiest to pick a variable with a coefficient of 1, if possible) 
2. Replace the isolated variable with its equivalent expression in the other equation 
3. Solve the equation found in step 2 
4. Plug in the value found in step 3 to the substitution equation from step 1 to find the value of the other variable

- ### Example: Solve the system
	- $2 x+y=3$ 
	- $4 x-3 y=1$
	- 1. In the first equation, the coefficient on y is 1, so solve for y to find: $$
y=-2 x+3
$$
2. Now, replace the y in the second equation with $-2x + 3$ to find: $4x−3(−2x+3)=1$

3. Now solve for x: $4x+6x−9=1$, so $10x=10$, and $x=1$

1. Finally, plug this value back into the substitution equation to solve for y:$y=-2(1)+3, y=1$
- One way to use **substitution** to solve two variable equations is to solve both equations for one value and then use substitution to set the two results equal to each other.
	- example:
		- $3s+t=16$
		- $2s+2t=10$
		- Solving the first equation for t, we get $t=16−3s$.   
		- Solving the second equation for t, we get $t=5−s$.
		- Solve the system by setting $16−3s=5-s$ and then solving.
