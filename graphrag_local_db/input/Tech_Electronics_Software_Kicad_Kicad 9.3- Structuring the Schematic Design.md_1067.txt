### Notes
- #### Structuring the Schematic Design in KiCad 9
- Schematic design in KiCad 9 begins with organizing components across multiple pages to ensure clarity and ease of navigation. This project consists of four schematic pages: the first page contains USB and power-related components, the second includes the ESP32 module and USB-to-serial converter, the third focuses on sensors, and the fourth is dedicated to the user interface.
- Components are placed logically to group related functions together. The USB connector, LiPo charger, voltage regulator, and battery connector are arranged in specific sections to improve readability. Supporting components such as resistors, capacitors, and LEDs are added systematically. The symbol chooser tool is used to search for and place components, ensuring that each part matches the bill of materials. KiCad shortcuts like the “A” key for adding components and “M” for moving elements improve workflow efficiency.
- Source: Lecture 11-110 - Starting with the Schematic Design

---
#### Implementing the LiPo Battery Charger Circuit
- The LiPo battery charger circuit follows the recommended design from the MCP73871 data sheet, ensuring proper wiring for charge management. The circuit includes pull-down resistors for programming charge parameters, as well as indicator LEDs for charge status. The resistors have specific values based on the manufacturer’s guidelines: R5 (2kΩ) and R6 (20kΩ) are connected to programming pins.
- LEDs provide visual feedback on battery status. A red, green, and blue LED indicate different charging states, connected via resistors to prevent overcurrent. The LEDs are powered by a USB 5V line, which is labeled accordingly to facilitate PCB layout. All ground connections are properly linked, and unused pins are either connected as recommended or left floating based on the data sheet instructions.
- Source: Lecture 12-120 - LiPo Battery Charger

---

#### Designing the USB Port Subcircuit
- The USB port subcircuit includes essential protective components to ensure safe operation. A polyfuse is placed on the VBus pin to prevent overcurrent damage. This fuse, sourced from BelFuse, provides automatic recovery in case of excessive current draw.
- An input filtering capacitor (0.1µF) is added to stabilize the power line and reduce voltage fluctuations. The capacitor is positioned strategically near the USB input. To ensure a clean schematic, unconnected pins on the USB connector are explicitly marked using the "No Connect" tool, preventing error messages in KiCad's Electrical Rules Check (ERC). This careful management of connections and labels improves the schematic’s clarity and reliability.
- Source: Lecture 13-130 - USB Port Subcircuit

---
#### Creating and Managing Nets for USB Data Signals
- Properly defining nets is crucial for organizing signal connections within a schematic. The net label tool is used to assign names to key connections, making it easier to identify signals during PCB layout. Differential pairs, such as USB DP (data positive) and DN (data negative), are labeled explicitly to facilitate proper routing later.
- The VBus power net is assigned to all relevant pins, ensuring that different parts of the circuit receive power from the same source. Labels such as "5V_USB" help in maintaining a structured design. Short-circuited differential pairs within the USB connector are also assigned appropriate labels to ensure correct high-speed signal handling in the PCB layout stage.
- Source: Lecture 14-140 - Create Nets

---
#### Finalizing the LiPo Charger Wiring and Power Management
- Completing the LiPo charger wiring involves properly labeling power and ground connections while ensuring correct voltage distribution. The charger’s VBat sense pin is shorted with VBat, and nets are assigned meaningful names for easy reference.
- To avoid ERC (Electrical Rules Check) errors, a power flag is added to the VSS pin to indicate its role as a power input. The input capacitor (1µF) is connected between the 5V USB line and ground, filtering any voltage spikes. The final schematic is enclosed within a labeled box, improving readability and documenting the function of the LiPo charger module within the overall design.
- Source: Lecture 15-150 - Finish LiPo Charger Design

---
#### Implementing the Voltage Regulator Circuit
- A low-dropout (LDO) voltage regulator converts 5V to 3.3V for powering the ESP32 and other components. The circuit follows the manufacturer’s guidelines, incorporating two filtering capacitors: a 0.1µF ceramic capacitor and a 10µF tantalum capacitor. The placement of these capacitors is critical, with the tantalum capacitor positioned closest to the regulator’s output pins to improve stability.
- A power flag is applied to input pins to prevent ERC warnings. Ground connections are managed carefully, ensuring clean signal paths. Consistent power labels are used across the schematic to maintain uniformity, with a dedicated 5V label replacing manual text-based labels for clarity. The completed voltage regulator block is enclosed within a labeled box for easier identification.
- Source: Lecture 16-160 - Voltage Regulator

---
#### Adding the Battery Connector and Power Indicator LED
- The battery connector is added and aligned with the existing LiPo charger circuit. It is rotated so that pins face inward for better wiring organization. The VBat net is connected to the charger, ensuring seamless power distribution.
- A power indicator LED is introduced to provide a visual confirmation of circuit operation. This LED is connected to the 3.3V output of the voltage regulator via a current-limiting resistor. A named net is assigned to this LED, making it easier to track in the layout. The battery connector and LED are enclosed in separate labeled boxes for clarity.
- Source: Lecture 17-170 - Battery Connector

---
#### Adding a Hierarchical Sheet for the ESP32 Module
- With the power-related components finalized, a hierarchical sheet is added to organize the ESP32 module and its associated circuitry separately. Using KiCad’s hierarchical sheet tool, a new schematic sheet is created and named "ESP32C3-02" to match the main component it contains.
- The hierarchical structure improves schematic readability and allows for better management of complex circuits. The new sheet appears in the project directory and within the KiCad schematic navigator, confirming its successful creation.
- Source: Lecture 18-180 - Add Hierarchical Sheet to Page 2

---
#### Refining the Schematic and Adjusting LED Connections
- Before proceeding to the ESP32 module, minor refinements are made to the first schematic page. The status LEDs are reviewed to ensure they correctly indicate battery charging states. The red LED is moved to indicate charging power, while the blue LED represents active charging, and the green LED indicates a fully charged battery.
- Labels are added to critical connections, improving readability during PCB layout. Any missing resistor values are updated, ensuring design accuracy. These refinements finalize the first schematic page, making it easier to transition to the next design stage.
- Source: Lecture 19-190 - Finish Work in Sheet 1
