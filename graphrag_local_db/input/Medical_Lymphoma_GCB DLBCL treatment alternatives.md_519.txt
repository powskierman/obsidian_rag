### Notes
-  For a relatively healthy 73-year-old patient with relapsed or refractory (R/R) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), including the germinal center B-cell-like (GCB) subtype, the choice between autologous stem cell transplantation (#ASCT) and CAR T-cell therapy depends on several factors, including the patient’s fitness, comorbidities, timing of relapse, and disease characteristics. Below is a comparison based on current evidence:
### Autologous Stem Cell Transplantation (#ASCT)
#### Advantages:
•	Standard of Care for Fit Patients: ==#ASCT has been the traditional approach for patients with chemosensitive R/R DLBCL who achieve remission after salvage chemotherapy.==
	•	Curative Potential: #ASCT can offer long-term remission in select patients.
	•	Well-Studied in Older Adults: ==Studies show that #ASCT is feasible in fit older adults up to age 75, provided they have no significant comorbidities.==
#### Disadvantages:
•	High Toxicity in Older Adults: Serious adverse events (Grade ≥3) are common in patients over 70, including cardiovascular complications, infections, and oral mucositis. The non-relapse mortality (NRM) rate is higher in older adults compared to younger patients.
	•	Modest Benefit in Early Relapse: ==For patients whose disease relapses within 12 months of first-line therapy, #ASCT outcomes are suboptimal compared to newer therapies like [[CAR-T Cell Therapy]] therapy==.
	•	Requires Chemosensitivity: #ASCT is only an option for patients who respond to salvage chemotherapy.
### [[CAR-T Cell Therapy]] Therapy
Three FDA-approved CAR-T cell therapies are most relevant for aggressive lymphomas, including HGBL:
1. Axicabtagene ciloleucel (Yescarta)
2. Tisagenlecleucel (Kymriah)
3. Lisocabtagene maraleucel (Breyanzi)
These therapies target CD19, a protein expressed on most B-cell lymphomas, including HGBL.
#### Advantages:
•	Superior Outcomes in Early Relapse: ==[[CAR-T Cell Therapy]] therapy has demonstrated better event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS) compared to #ASCT in patients whose disease relapses within 12 months of first-line treatment.==
	•	Comparable Efficacy Across Age Groups: Studies show that older adults (≥65 or even ≥70 years) achieve similar response rates and progression-free survival (PFS) as younger patients with CAR T-cell therapy. This includes patients with high-risk features like GCB DLBCL.
	•	Tolerability in Older Adults: While CAR T-cell therapy carries risks such as cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and neurotoxicity (ICANS), these are generally manageable even in older patients. Non-relapse mortality rates are lower compared to #ASCT in older adults.
#### Disadvantages:
•	Toxicity Risks: CRS and ICANS can be life-threatening if not managed properly. However, these risks are not significantly higher in older adults compared to younger ones.
•	Cost and Accessibility: [[CAR-T Cell Therapy]] therapy is expensive and may not be readily available at all centers.
•	Limited Long-Term Data: While CAR T-cell therapy offers promising results, long-term follow-up data is still evolving.
#### Key Evidence for Older Adults
1.	A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials showed that [[CAR-T Cell Therapy]] therapy significantly improved EFS and OS compared to #ASCT for R/R large B-cell lymphoma relapsing within 12 months, without excess toxicity.
	2.	Subgroup analyses from trials like ==ZUMA-7 demonstrated that CAR T-cell therapy is effective and tolerable in older adults (>65 years), with improved quality of life scores compared to standard salvage chemotherapy followed by #ASCT.==
	3.	==Retrospective studies comparing [[CAR-T Cell Therapy]] therapy and #ASCT in older adults found similar outcomes but lower non-relapse mortality rates with [[CAR-T Cell Therapy]] therapy, suggesting it may be a safer option for patients aged ≥70 years.==
#### Recommendation
For a healthy 74-year-old patient:
	•	==If the disease relapsed within 12 months of first-line therapy or if there are concerns about tolerating high-dose chemotherapy due to age-related risks, [[CAR-T Cell Therapy]] therapy is likely the better choice, offering superior outcomes with manageable toxicity.
	•	If the disease relapsed after more than 12 months and the patient has no significant comorbidities or frailty concerns, #ASCT remains a viable curative option, provided the patient achieves chemosensitivity with salvage chemotherapy.==
Ultimately, the decision should involve a multidisciplinary team assessment of the patient’s overall health, comorbidities, caregiver support, and preferences.
