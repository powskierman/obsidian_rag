### Notes
-  you can simulate the behavior of activating a switch in CircuitLab by automating the switch between charging and discharging the capacitor.
![[Tech/Electronics/media/wiring.png]]

I've been figuring out how to do this with CircuitLab for a long time. I'm sharing it with you in the hope that you'll find it useful.

The trick is to set up a control system for each of the push buttons that respectively control the charging and discharging of the capacitor:

![[Electronics/media/circuitlab-circuit.png]]
In CircuitLab, a periodic signal can control some rather unique buttons. In our case, a periodic square wave signal will enable us to close and open each button at regular intervals at the desired frequency.

We can easily calculate the time constant of the RC components in our circuit. We have C = 10 µF and R1 = 10 kΩ. We can, therefore, calculate τ = R x C = 1 s. We also know that the capacitor is charged once the time 5 x τ has elapsed, i.e., after 5 s. We'll therefore choose to toggle the buttons that control current flow in the circuit after a time greater than 5 s. For example, let's set a value of 6.25 s. In other words, if we generate a square-wave signal with a period of 2 x 6.25 = 12.5 s, i.e., a frequency of 1 / 12.5 = 80 mHz, we're sure not to trigger the capacitor charging and discharging too soon.

We'll also need to introduce a phase shift between the V1 and V2 signals so that the flip-flops alternate correctly and trigger the charges and discharges alternately without interfering.

Let's set these parameters in CircuitLab:

![[Electronics/media/circuitlab-switch-settings.png]]
We have now configured our circuit control:

![[Electronics/media/circuitlab-switch-controls.png]]
We can now parameterize the simulation in the time domain:

![[Electronics/media/circuitlab-controlled-switches.png]]
Here, we'd like to measure the electrical potential of node A5 and the intensity of currents I1 and I2 flowing, respectively, in the circuit's charging (through R1) and discharging (through R2) meshes. We'd also like to compare the variation of these signals with that of the square-wave push-button control signals.

We can now run the simulation:

![[Electronics/media/circuitlab-controlled-simulation.png]])
The circuit's behavior during capacitor charging and discharging can then be observed perfectly.
