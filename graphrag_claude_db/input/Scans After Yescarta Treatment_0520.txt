### Notes
- After your CAR-T cell infusion, these two imaging tests will provide critical information about your response to treatment. Here's what you can expect from each:

### CT Scan (1 Month Post-Infusion)

**Purpose:**
- Early assessment of disease response
- Primarily evaluating size changes in your retroperitoneal mass
- Checking for any complications from treatment

**Possible Outcomes:**
1. **Size reduction** - Early sign of response, though the mass likely won't completely disappear yet
2. **Stable disease** - No significant change in size (common at this early stage)
3. **Pseudoprogression** - Temporary increase in size due to inflammation and immune cell infiltration (can happen with CAR-T therapy and doesn't necessarily indicate treatment failure)

**What Happens Next:**
- This is primarily an interim assessment
- Your medical team will use this information to guide immediate management
- They typically won't make definitive treatment decisions based solely on this scan

### PET Scan (3 Months Post-Infusion)

**Purpose:**
- Definitive assessment of metabolic response
- Evaluating both size and metabolic activity of your retroperitoneal mass
- This is the key timepoint for determining treatment success

**Possible Outcomes:**
1. **Complete metabolic response (CMR)** - No abnormal FDG uptake (Deauville score 1-3)
   - This is the optimal outcome suggesting successful treatment
   - Associated with long-term remission in many patients

2. **Partial metabolic response (PMR)** - Significant reduction in FDG uptake but some activity remains
   - May lead to continued observation or consideration of consolidation therapy

3. **No metabolic response (NMR)** or **Progressive metabolic disease (PMD)**
   - Would likely prompt discussion of additional treatment options (like the radiation therapy or bispecific antibodies you've asked about)

**What Happens Next:**
- If complete response: transition to surveillance
- If partial response: possible consolidation or close observation
- If no response/progression: discussion of salvage options

Your oncologist will discuss the findings from these scans with you and develop the appropriate next steps based on your specific results and overall clinical picture.
