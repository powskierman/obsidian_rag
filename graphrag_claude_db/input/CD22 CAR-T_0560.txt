### Notes
- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">A CAR-T CD22 clinical trial is currently open to patients in Calgary</mark> as part of a Canada-wide research initiative targeting relapsed or refractory B-cell malignancies. The trial, called <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">CLIC-2201</mark>, is a phase I, multicenter study sponsored by the BC Cancer Agency and includes both pediatric and adult patient cohorts at several sites, including Calgary. The trial evaluates a “Made-in-Canada” CD22 CAR T-cell product, which is <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">particularly valuable for those who are resistant to prior CD19 CAR-T therapy</mark>, and manufacturing is coordinated through the Conconi Family Immunotherapy Laboratory in BC .
Overview of the Calgary Trial
	•	<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">The CLIC-2201 trial is actively recruiting in Calgary</mark> for relapsed or refractory B-cell lymphoma and acute lymphoblastic leukemia, including pediatric and young adult candidates .
	•	Participants undergo leukapheresis in Calgary, with cell processing performed in BC prior to re-infusion locally .
	•	The research is led by Dr. Kevin Hay, who continues to advance this work in Calgary, with support from the Canadian Institutes of Health Research and BioCanRx .
	•	CLIC-2201 is distinct for using nanobody-based CARs, a cutting-edge approach developed by a Canadian public-private partnership .
Who Might Qualify
	•	Eligible: Calgary-based patients (both adult and pediatric) with relapsed or refractory B-cell malignancies.
	•	Ineligible: Patients with conditions outside the scope of the trial, such as solid tumors or non-B-cell cancers .
How to Get More Information
	•	Discuss eligibility and referral specifics with Dr. Slaby.
	•	The BC Cancer Immunotherapy Program and BioCanRx are coordinating the trial nationwide; Calgary is confirmed as a point-of-care recruitment and treatment site .
	•	For direct information or collaborative inquiries, consider contacting the Immunotherapy Program Project Manager at BC Cancer (ImmRxinfo@bccrc.ca) .
Further Reading
	•	Calgary-specific trial site information is visible on Canadian cancer trial listings, for example, at CenterWatch and on the BioCanRx dashboard .
	•	The Charbonneau Riddell Centre for Cancer Immunotherapy at the University of Calgary is involved in related research and clinical delivery of CAR-T therapies .
This Calgary trial offers a much-needed alternative for patients who are not responding to available CAR-T therapies and represents a major step forward for cell therapy innovation across Alberta. For those in Alberta needing options beyond CD19-targeted treatments, this trial can provide access to the newest generation of Canadian-developed CAR-T cell therapy.
