The charge of a particle tells us how it interacts with the field that carries the associated force. So electrons, which have electric charge –1, interact directly with photons, which carry the electromagnetic force; neutrinos, which have electric charge 0, don’t interact directly with photons at all. (They can interact indirectly, since neutrinos interact with electrons, which then interact with photons.) Photons are neutral themselves, so they don’t interact directly with one another. — location: [6632](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B014EOUMZA&location=6632) ^ref-60819

All of the fermions we know about have a weak nuclear charge, so they interact with W and Z bosons. Half of the fermions we know about interact with the gluons that carry the strong force, and we call those fermions quarks; the other half do not, and we call them leptons. There are up-type quarks, with (electric) charge +2/3, and down-type quarks, with charge –1/3. The strong force is so strong that quarks and gluons are confined inside particles like protons and neutrons, so we never see them directly. — location: [6637](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B014EOUMZA&location=6637) ^ref-29597
