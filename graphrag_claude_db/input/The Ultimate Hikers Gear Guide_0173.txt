# Highlights

Complete sun and moon data for U.S. cities and worldwide waypoints is available at the Naval Oceanography Portal, at the U.S. Naval Meteorology and Oceanography Command website. ([327](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=327)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464951

---

When it’s really buggy, I wear a nylon long-sleeve shirt and nylon trekking pants. Insects struggle to bite through the tight weave, and a permethrin-based treatment like Insect Shield is an added deterrent. ([551](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=551)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464952

---

If it is not buggy, I wear either polyester or merino wool. ([558](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=558)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464953

---

For a backpacking trip, however, I prefer merino wool. ([560](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=560)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464954

---

In temperatures above 50°F or so, I wear a short-sleeve, lightweight merino wool shirt, unless the sun exposure is considerable or I am traveling off trail, in which case I wear a lightweight long-sleeve to better protect my arms. I like both shirts to have chest zippers and a long torso so that they won’t ride up and cause my lower back to chafe. ([560](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=560)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464955

---

The Patagonia Nano Puff Hoody ($200, 14 oz) is the current gold standard for three-season puffy jackets. It has ultralight shell fabrics and top-of-the-line PrimaLoft One insulation. The MontBell U.L. Therma-wrap Pants ($145, 10 oz) share a similar function-driven philosophy. ([714](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=714)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464956

---

My preferred liner is the DeFeet Merino Wool DuraGlove ($18.50, 3 oz), which has a longer life span than any other glove I have tried, thanks to a grippy silicone pattern and 20 percent nylon content. ([1010](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1010)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464957

---

I like the RBH Designs Vapor Mitt ($145, 9 oz) for its durability and warmth. The RBH Designs Ultralight Vapor Mitt ($145, 6 oz) is suitable for less cold conditions. ([1028](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1028)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464958

---

At night, after my feet have dried out, I coat the bottoms with a hydrophobic balm such as Hydropel ([1184](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1184)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464959

---

For on-trail trips, my current favorite is the La Sportiva Electron trail-running shoe ($115, 13 oz), which protects my underfoot with a plush rock-absorbing outsole. ([1215](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1215)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464960

---

For winter conditions non-waterproof trail-running shoes are not suitable—they are not warm enough because they will get soaked from snow. Instead, I wear a mid-cut waterproof/breathable lightweight boot like the La Sportiva FC 3.0 GTX ($150, 18 oz), which will dry out faster than a classic full-grain leather model. ([1223](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1223)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464961

---

Usually, I wear only a liner—specifically, the DeFeet Wooleator ($12.50, 2 oz per pair), which is thin and unpadded; it’s made of 63 percent merino wool, 36 percent nylon, and 1 percent spandex. ([1277](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1277)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464962

---

In limited situations, I may use a more traditional, thicker hiking sock, like the DeFeet EcoTrekker ($15, 3 oz), which has a mix of materials identical to the Wooleator. The EcoTrekker absorbs more water and does not dry as quickly as a thinner sock, but it provides more insulation in cold, wet conditions. ([1293](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1293)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464963

---

On long-term trips in frigid temperatures (i.e., never above 15°F), I use a three-component system: > As a next-to-skin liner, I use the DeFeet Wooleator Hi Top ($13, 2 oz), which is identical to the Wooleator except for a higher ankle cuff. > I wear a thin vapor barrier liner sock from RBH Designs or Integral Designs; see the section on vapor barrier liners (this page–this page) in the Clothing chapter for details and an explanation of the technology. > The EcoTrekker is worn over the first two layers, for warmth. ([1297](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1297)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464964

---

A better option is the Simblissity LevaGaiter ($27, 2 oz per pair), which fit without the need for an instep strap or cord and which cost less and breathe and ventilate better than conventional gaiters. ([1323](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1323)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464965

---

Tip: Shelter walls or floor leaking? Restore polyurethane-coated fabrics with McNett’s Tent Sure sealant, and silicone-impregnated nylon with Atsko Silicone Water Guard. ([1631](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0078XD4AA&location=1631)) %% Color: yellow %% ^334464966
