### Notes
- Encapsulated bacteria are bacteria surrounded by a polysaccharide capsule that acts as a protective layer. This capsule is important to understand in the context of your CAR T-cell therapy because it relates to your increased infection risk.

Key encapsulated bacteria include:

1. **Streptococcus pneumoniae** - Common cause of pneumonia, sinusitis, ear infections, and meningitis
2. **Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib)** - Can cause severe respiratory infections and meningitis
3. **Neisseria meningitidis** - Causes meningococcal meningitis and septicemia
4. **Klebsiella pneumoniae** - Can cause pneumonia, urinary tract infections, and septicemia
5. **Salmonella typhi** - Causes typhoid fever

These bacteria are particularly relevant to me for several reasons:

- The capsule shields these bacteria from my immune system's normal defenses
- Antibodies (which I'll have fewer of due to B-cell aplasia) are crucial for fighting these specific bacteria
- People with impaired B-cell function or hypogammaglobulinemia are at higher risk for these infections

My medical team will :
- Monitor me closely for signs of infection
- Consider prophylactic antibiotics in some cases
- ==Potentially recommend vaccinations against encapsulated bacteria before treatment== (though this is often done before CAR T-cell collection)
- Consider immunoglobulin replacement therapy (IVIG) if my antibody levels become significantly reduced

If I develop fever or signs of infection during or after treatment, it's important to seek medical attention promptly, as these infections can progress rapidly in immunocompromised individuals.
