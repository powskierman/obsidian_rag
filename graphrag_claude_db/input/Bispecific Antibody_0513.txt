### Notes
-  Bispecific antibody (BsAb) treatments represent a promising new approach for patients with high-grade B-cell lymphoma (HGBCL), particularly in relapsed or refractory settings. These therapies work by engaging the patient’s immune system, specifically T-cells, to target and destroy lymphoma cells.
- How Bispecific Antibodies Work
	•	BsAbs are engineered molecules designed to bind two different targets simultaneously:
	•	One arm binds to a tumor-associated antigen, such as CD20 on B-cell lymphoma cells.
	•	The other arm binds to CD3 on T-cells, activating them and directing them to attack the lymphoma cells.
	•	This mechanism bypasses the need for major histocompatibility complex (MHC) presentation, making it effective even in immune-evasive tumors.
- Approved Bispecific Antibodies for HGBCL
	1.	[[Epcoritamab]] (Epkinly):
		•	FDA-approved in 2023 for relapsed/refractory HGBCL and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) after two or more lines of systemic therapy.
		•	Administered subcutaneously, it offers a convenient “off-the-shelf” option compared to CAR T-cell therapy.
		•	Common side effects include cytokine release syndrome (CRS), fatigue, and infections.  
		- epcoritamab (Epkinly) is authorized for use in Alberta under specific conditions. Here are the key details:
		- Authorization Status:
	1. **Health Canada Approval**
	        - Conditionally approved (October 2023) for relapsed/refractory DLBCL, high-grade B-cell lymphoma (HGBCL), and other aggressive B-cell lymphomas after ≥2 prior therapies in patients who:        
	        - Previously received CAR T-cell therapy **or**
	        - Are ineligible for CAR T-cell therapy [2](https://www.abbvie.ca/content/dam/abbvie-dotcom/ca/en/documents/press-releases/EPKINLY_PR_NOC_EN.pdf)[4](https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/RDS1699032842238)[6](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/notice-compliance/conditions/qualifying-notice-epkinly-271331.html).
	2. **Alberta Reimbursement**
	     - Publicly funded in Alberta since October 2024 under a **pan-Canadian accelerated access program** [7](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/nine-provinces-approve-accelerated-public-reimbursement-of-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma-treatment-under-new-early-access-process-801364235.html).
	    - Coverage applies to patients meeting Health Canada’s criteria, with prescribing restricted to hematologists/oncologists [5](https://www.cda-amc.ca/sites/default/files/DRR/2024/PC0334_Epkinly-Final_Rec.pdf)[8](https://www.cda-amc.ca/sites/default/files/DRR/2024/PC0334-Combined_Review.pdf).
- Key Conditions for Use
	- **Prior Therapy Requirements**:    
	    - ≥2 systemic treatments (e.g., R-CHOP, platinum-based regimens)
	    - CAR T-cell therapy attempted or deemed unsuitable (e.g., rapid progression, comorbidities, lack of access) [4](https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/RDS1699032842238)[8](https://www.cda-amc.ca/sites/default/files/DRR/2024/PC0334-Combined_Review.pdf).
	    - **Administration**:
	     - Subcutaneous injection with mandatory premedication (steroids, antihistamines, acetaminophen) to mitigate cytokine release syndrome (CRS) risks [5](https://www.cda-amc.ca/sites/default/files/DRR/2024/PC0334_Epkinly-Final_Rec.pdf)[9](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/abbvie-s-epkinly-tm-epcoritamab-injection-epcoritamab-for-injection-receives-health-canada-authorization-with-conditions-as-the-first-and-only-subcutaneous-bispecific-antibody-to-treat-adult-patients-with-relapsed-or-refractory-diffuse-larg-863040377.html).
	     - **Clinical Context**
	     - **Efficacy**: 63% overall response rate (40% complete responses) in refractory DLBCL/HGBCL, including CAR T-resistant cases [2](https://www.abbvie.ca/content/dam/abbvie-dotcom/ca/en/documents/press-releases/EPKINLY_PR_NOC_EN.pdf)[5](https://www.cda-amc.ca/sites/default/files/DRR/2024/PC0334_Epkinly-Final_Rec.pdf)- **Alberta Access**: Part of a coordinated provincial rollout to address urgent unmet needs in aggressive lymphomas [7](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/nine-provinces-approve-accelerated-public-reimbursement-of-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma-treatment-under-new-early-access-process-801364235.html).
For eligibility confirmation, Alberta clinicians must submit treatment requests through provincial oncology pathways.
	2.	Glofitamab (Columvi):
	•	Approved for relapsed/refractory DLBCL and has shown efficacy in HGBCL as well.
	•	Administered intravenously with a step-up dosing schedule to reduce the risk of CRS.
	3.	Mosunetuzumab:
	•	Initially approved for follicular lymphoma but being explored in DLBCL and HGBCL settings.
	•	Demonstrates significant activity as a single agent or in combination therapies.
Clinical Efficacy
	•	BsAbs have shown impressive response rates in heavily pretreated patients:
	•	Overall response rates (ORR) range from 50% to 70%, with complete response (CR) rates of approximately 30%-40% in some studies.
	•	These therapies are particularly valuable for patients who relapse after CAR T-cell therapy or are not eligible for CAR T-cell treatment due to age, comorbidities, or logistical challenges.
Advantages of BsAbs
	•	Off-the-Shelf Availability: Unlike CAR T-cell therapy, which requires personalized manufacturing, BsAbs are ready-to-use.
	•	Broad Applicability: Suitable for patients who cannot tolerate intensive treatments like stem cell transplantation or CAR T-cell therapy.
	•	Manageable Toxicity: While CRS is a common side effect, it is generally milder than that seen with CAR T-cell therapy and can be managed with supportive care and medications like tocilizumab.
Future Directions
	•	Ongoing trials are exploring BsAbs in earlier lines of treatment, as well as in combination with chemotherapy or other immunotherapies.
	•	Research is also focused on optimizing dosing schedules and identifying biomarkers to predict response and resistance.
Conclusion
Bispecific antibodies like epcoritamab and glofitamab offer an effective and less intensive alternative for treating relapsed/refractory HGBCL. They are particularly beneficial for patients who have failed multiple lines of therapy or are not candidates for CAR T-cell therapy. With continued advancements, BsAbs are expected to play an increasingly significant role in the management of aggressive lymphomas like HGBCL.
