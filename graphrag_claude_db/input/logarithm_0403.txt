- natural logarithms are the inverse of natural exponentiation
- Logarithm laws:
	-  $\log_a(zw)=\log_a z + \log_a w$
	- $\log_a{z \over w} = \log_a z - \log_a w$
	- $log_a z^k = k \log_a z$
- Logarithms decrease complexity
	- ##### Product Rule
		- $log(a) + log(b) = log(ab)$
		- multiplication becomes addition
	- ##### Quotient Rule
		- $log(a) - log(b) =log(a/b)$
		- division becomes substraction
	- ##### Power Rule
		- exponentiation becomes multiplication
		- $log(a)^b = (b) (log(a))$
- Logarithms reduce all exponential expressions to natural logarithms and exponentials
	- $a^b=e^{b \ln a}$ (see )
	- $x = a^{\log_a x}$ so $x = e^{ln x}$
	-  Since $2^5 =32$ and $3^7 =2,187$ we have:
		- $\log_2(32) = \log_2(2^5) = 5$
		- $\log_3(2,187) = \log_3(3^7) = 7$
- To workout log of any base, $\log_a x= \ln x/\ln a$
-  Using properties of logarithms, we quickly simplify the following expression:
	- $\log(\sqrt 1000) = \log (1,000^{1/2}) = {1 \over 2}\log(1,000) = {1 \over 2}\log (10^3) = {3 \over 2}$
