### Notes
- 1. Top-Level Categories

    Subjects: Create high-level categories for each mathematical subject (e.g., Algebra, Geometry, Trigonometry, Calculus, Logarithms). This will allow you to quickly navigate between different areas of mathematics.
        Example:

        # Mathematics
        ## Algebra
        ## Geometry
        ## Trigonometry
        ## Calculus
        ## Logarithms

2. Subcategories and Sub-Subcategories

    Within each subject, break down the content into smaller, more specific subcategories. This hierarchical structure will help you drill down to the exact information you need.
        Example under Algebra:

        # Mathematics
        ## Algebra
        ### Linear Equations
        ### Quadratic Equations
        ### Systems of Equations

3. Reference Section

    Designate a section for reference materials such as PDFs, cheat sheets, and online resources. This will serve as a go-to place for supplementary information.
        Example:

        # Mathematics
        ## References
        ### Algebra References
        ### Geometry References
        ### Calculus References

4. Tags or Keywords

    Use tags or keywords to further categorize your notes. This will enhance searchability and allow you to filter content based on specific topics.
        Example:

        # Mathematics
        ## Algebra [Tag: algebra, linear_equations]
        ## Geometry [Tag: geometry, triangles]

5. Notes by Concept

    Group related notes together under a single heading to explore concepts in depth. This will help you understand the relationships between different ideas.
        Example:

        # Mathematics
        ## Trigonometry
        ### Sine, Cosine, and Tangent Functions [Note: unit_circle.md]
        ### Trigonometric Identities [Note: trigonometric_identities.md]

6. Headings and Subheadings

    Use Markdown-style headings to break up your notes into clear sections. This will improve readability and make it easier to scan through your content.
        Example:

        # Calculus
        ## Limits and Derivatives
        ### Definition of a Limit
        ### Properties of Limits

7. Mind Mapping

    Consider using a mind mapping approach to create visual representations of your notes. This will help you see the relationships between different mathematical concepts.
        Example:

        # Mathematics Mind Map
        ## Algebra
        ### Linear Equations
        #### Slope-Intercept Form
        #### Point-Slope Form

Conclusion

By structuring your notes in this manner, you will be able to efficiently organize and retrieve information, making your study process more effective. This approach draws on the suggestions provided in the context, ensuring that your notes are both comprehensive and easy to navigate. Keep up the great work, and happy note-taking!
