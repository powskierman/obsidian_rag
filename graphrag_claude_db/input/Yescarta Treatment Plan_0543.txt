- ### Notes
- I am undergoing Yescarta (axicabtagene ciloleucel) therapy for double-hit GC-DLBCL (germinal center diffuse large B-cell lymphoma). Having just completed T-cell collection (apheresis), here's what I can expect in the coming weeks:

### Post-T-cell Collection (Where I am Now)
- I may experience some fatigue or bruising at the collection site
- My medical team will be sending my T-cells to be engineered with CAR (chimeric antigen receptor) that targets CD19 on lymphoma cells
- This manufacturing process typically takes 2-3 weeks (17 days)

### Pre-infusion Phase
- I will receive lympho-depleting chemotherapy (typically fludarabine and cyclophosphamide) for 3 days
- Purpose: to create "space" for the CAR T-cells to expand
- Side effects may include nausea, fatigue, and temporary blood count reduction

### CAR T-cell Infusion
- One-time infusion through IV (typically takes less than 30 minutes)
- I'll be monitored closely during infusion
- Generally well-tolerated but I may experience fever, chills, or low blood pressure

### Post-infusion Monitoring (Weeks 1-4)
- Hospital stay required for 14 days for monitoring
- **Key potential side effects to monitor:
  1. Cytokine Release Syndrome (==CRS==) - typically occurs days 1-5 (Frequency: ==35.18%==)
     - Fever, low blood pressure, oxygen needs
     - Treated with tocilizumab and/or steroids if needed
  2. Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (==ICANS==) - typically days 5-14 (Frequency: ==19.87%==) 
     - Confusion, difficulty speaking, seizures
     - Managed with steroids and supportive care
 3. neurotoxicity and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (==HLH): 14.98%==

### Longer-term Recovery (Months 1-6)
- Ongoing monitoring of blood counts
- Potential for prolonged cytopenias (low blood counts)
- Increased infection risk due to B-cell aplasia and hypogammaglobulinemia
- Quarterly follow-up scans to assess response

### Important Practical Considerations
- You'll need a caregiver available 24/7 for at least 4 weeks after infusion
- Must stay within 2 hours of the treatment facility for at least 4 weeks
- Keep thermometer handy to monitor for fever
- Avoid driving during the first month due to potential neurological effects

I recommend discussing specific timing and monitoring protocols with your healthcare team, as they may have particular approaches based on your individual case and their institutional practices.
