- #### Understanding PCB Design
- PCB design is a structured process that requires careful planning and execution. The IoT development board used in this project serves as an example of a medium-complexity PCB, designed with a four-layer structure and a variety of integrated circuits. The use of KiCad 9 for this design allows for an efficient workflow, from schematic design to layout and manufacturing. One of the key aspects of designing such a board is component selection, ensuring that each part meets the functional and design requirements. The placement of components, such as capacitors, connectors, and integrated circuits, plays a crucial role in the board’s performance and manufacturability.
- Another fundamental consideration is signal integrity, which involves proper grounding, power distribution, and noise reduction techniques. The inclusion of a bill of materials (BOM) helps keep track of all necessary components and their sources. Designing a board like this typically takes one to two days for an experienced engineer, but the process can be streamlined through good planning and organization. The version of KiCad used in this course is KiCad 9 RC1, which is nearly identical to the final release, ensuring that students can follow along without discrepancies between software versions.
- Source: Lecture 01-010 - Introduction to PCB Design with KiCad 9

#### Selecting Components and Creating a Bill of Materials
- Component selection is a fundamental step in PCB design, requiring careful consideration of availability, functionality, and compatibility. The IoT board used in this course includes the ESP32-C3S2 microcontroller, SD card module, flash memory, and several sensors, such as the BME280 for environmental monitoring, an ambient light sensor, and a microphone. The board also features a USB-C connector for power and data, integrated circuits for serial communication, power regulation circuitry, and a battery management system.
- The complexity of the board necessitates a four-layer PCB structure, ensuring proper signal routing and power distribution. The bill of materials (BOM) is an essential document that lists all components, their specifications, and sources. Maintaining an organized BOM ensures that all components are accounted for before manufacturing, reducing the likelihood of delays due to unavailable parts. By following a structured approach, designers can transition smoothly from component selection to schematic design and PCB layout, ultimately preparing the board for manufacturing.

#### Key Considerations in Schematic Design and PCB Layout
- Schematic design is the foundation of a successful PCB, determining how components are electrically connected. One of the critical aspects of schematic design is signal conditioning, which involves using decoupling capacitors near power pins to stabilize voltage and reduce noise. Pull-up and pull-down resistors are also necessary for reliable communication in circuits, such as I²C buses.
- The selection and placement of capacitors of varying values help manage different types of noise—high-frequency noise is handled by ceramic capacitors, while tantalum capacitors manage low-frequency fluctuations. Component placement in the layout stage follows manufacturer guidelines to ensure proper operation. In the case of the ESP32, placement is particularly important to prevent interference with its integrated antenna.
- Another consideration is component sourcing. While basic components like resistors are readily available in KiCad’s libraries, more complex parts such as USB connectors and buttons may need to be sourced externally. Using distributors like SnapMagic ensures that components are available and suitable for the manufacturing process. Including 3D models in the PCB design also aids in visualizing the final board, allowing designers to confirm component placement and avoid mechanical conflicts.

#### Organizing a PCB Project with a Multipage Schematic
- A multipage schematic is an effective way to manage complex PCB designs. Instead of placing all components on a single schematic page, a hierarchical structure improves readability and organization. In this project, the schematic is divided into several sections, including power management, microcontroller and memory, sensors, and user interface components. Each section is placed on a separate page, making it easier to navigate and troubleshoot the design.
- The four-layer PCB layout is structured for optimal performance. The top and bottom layers are primarily used for signal routing, while the first inner layer serves as a ground plane to reduce electrical noise. The second inner layer is used for the 3.3V power plane, ensuring clean power distribution.
- Track width selection is another key design decision. Power traces are made wider to handle higher currents efficiently, while signal traces are kept thinner to optimize space. Copper zones further improve thermal performance and power delivery. Early planning in component selection, layout decisions, and data sheet research significantly contributes to a high-quality and cost-effective PCB.

#### Prioritizing Design Requirements for a Functional PCB
- Designing a PCB requires balancing technical needs with real-world constraints. Three primary requirements guided the development of the IoT board in this project. The first requirement was to create an educational project that demonstrates PCB design in KiCad 9. The second was to incorporate a modern microcontroller with wireless connectivity and sufficient processing power, along with a diverse set of components requiring a four-layer PCB. The final requirement was to ensure that the board could be successfully manufactured by NextPCB.
- Planning ahead before starting the design process is essential. This includes gathering component data sheets, selecting footprints and symbols, and ensuring that chosen components are available for manufacturing. These priorities influenced every aspect of the design, from component placement and power distribution to signal routing and layout optimization.
- Source: Lecture 05-050 - Design Priorities

#### A Detailed Walkthrough of the Board’s Components
- The ESP32-C3 was chosen as the central microcontroller due to its high processing power and built-in wireless capabilities. The board includes multiple power options, supporting both USB-C and battery operation, each with dedicated power management circuits.
- Data storage is a critical aspect of the design. The SD card module allows for local data storage, enabling the board to log large amounts of data independently. Complementing this, the flash memory chip provides fast, non-volatile storage for firmware and configuration data. These two storage methods work together to offer flexibility in data handling.
- Component placement was carefully planned, particularly for the ESP32, which has strict placement guidelines due to its integrated antenna. The positioning of connectors and buttons around the edges improves usability, while grouping related components enhances organization.
- Source: Lecture 06-060 - Component Walkthrough

#### Sourcing and Managing Components Efficiently
- Gathering all necessary components before starting a PCB design helps streamline the workflow. This involves researching available parts, collecting data sheets, and ensuring that symbols, footprints, and 3D models are ready for use in KiCad. Despite thorough planning, some design changes may be required, as seen with the USB connector in this project, which had to be replaced due to availability and fit issues.
- Online resources such as SnapMagic, Digi-Key, and SnapEDA are invaluable for finding and verifying components. Keeping track of sourcing information in KiCad’s schematic editor ensures that components are correctly documented and available for manufacturing.
- Reading and following data sheets is crucial for proper component integration. Manufacturer recommendations provide guidelines for wiring, placement, and supporting circuitry, ensuring that the PCB functions as intended. By taking the time to source components and understand their requirements, designers can reduce errors and avoid delays in the manufacturing process.
- Source: Lecture 07-070 - Component Sources and Collection
