### Notes
- Here is a list of novel agents targeting MYC and BCL-2, along with detailed references:

---

### **Agents Targeting MYC**
1. **JMBI-001**:
   - A synthetic lethal small-molecule inhibitor targeting MKLP2, which exploits vulnerabilities created by MYC overexpression.
   - Demonstrates selective killing of MYC-driven cancer cells while sparing normal cells.
   - Preclinical studies show excellent efficacy and safety profiles, and clinical trials are planned soon[1].

2. **GT19630**:
   - A molecular glue that degrades MYC and GSPT1, promoting apoptosis, blocking cell cycle progression, and enhancing immune response by degrading immune checkpoint protein B7-H3.
   - Effective at low nanomolar concentrations in preclinical models[2].

3. **OTX-2002**:
   - A MYC-targeted Epigenomic Controller (MYC-EC) delivered via lipid nanoparticles to downregulate MYC expression pre-transcriptionally.
   - Demonstrates durable epigenomic changes and tumor suppression in a first-in-human Phase I/II study[3].

4. **OMO-103**:
   - A mini-protein designed to directly inhibit MYC by penetrating cells and targeting the nucleus.
   - Early clinical trials have shown safety and promising anti-tumor activity in heavily pretreated patients[8].

5. **MYCN/MDM2 Dual Inhibition**:
   - Targeting the MYCN oncogene alongside MDM2 has shown promise in preclinical models for neuroblastoma and other cancers.
   - This approach leverages the positive feedback loop between MYCN and MDM2 for enhanced therapeutic outcomes[7].

---

### **Agents Targeting BCL-2**
1. **Venetoclax (ABT-199)**:
   - A selective BCL-2 inhibitor approved for treating hematologic malignancies like chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) and acute myeloid leukemia (AML).
   - Often used in combination therapies to enhance efficacy.

2. **Lisaftoclax (APG-2575)**:
   - A next-generation BCL-2 inhibitor currently in Phase I/II trials for hematologic cancers.
   - Designed to overcome resistance seen with venetoclax[5].

3. **Navitoclax (ABT-263)**:
   - Targets both BCL-2 and BCL-XL but has limited use due to thrombocytopenia side effects.
   - Combination therapies are being explored to improve tolerability and efficacy[5][9].

4. **AZD0466**:
   - A dual inhibitor of BCL-2 and BCL-XL in Phase I/II trials for hematologic cancers and solid tumors.
   - Uses selective drug delivery strategies to minimize toxicities[5][9].

5. **Combination Therapies**:
   - Venetoclax combined with BTK inhibitors (e.g., acalabrutinib) or chemotherapy agents like asparaginase has shown deep responses in resistant leukemia cases[4][10].

6. **PROTACs**:
   - Proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs) are being developed to selectively degrade BCL-X L or MCL1, offering tumor-specific inhibition with reduced toxicity[9].

---

### **Comments**
These agents represent cutting-edge advancements in targeting MYC and BCL-2, two critical oncogenic pathways in aggressive cancers like double-hit lymphoma. While some are still in early clinical development, they provide hope for overcoming resistance to traditional therapies such as chemotherapy or CAR T-cell therapy. Discuss these options with your oncologist to explore their relevance to your treatment plan.
