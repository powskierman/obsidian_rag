#### Expanding the Schematic to Include the ESP32 and Peripherals
- The second schematic sheet contains the circuitry related to the ESP32 module, USB-to-UART bridge, flash memory, and the SD card module. These components are critical for communication, storage, and microcontroller functionality. The hierarchical structure of the project allows each major functional block to be placed on separate pages for better organization.
- To begin this section, the primary components are added to the schematic. The ESP32 microcontroller, the USB-to-UART bridge (CP2102), an electrostatic discharge (ESD) suppressor, an SPI flash memory module, and the SD card module are positioned and labeled. Assigning correct designators at this stage is essential to maintain consistency throughout the schematic and avoid errors during PCB layout. Each component’s placement is aligned with the bill of materials to ensure a structured approach to the design.

#### Integrating the USB-to-UART Bridge and ESD Protection
- The USB-to-UART bridge (CP2102) enables serial communication between a computer and the ESP32, facilitating programming and debugging. This chip converts USB signals to UART signals while also handling USB power delivery. The ESD suppressor (USBLC6) is included alongside the bridge to protect against voltage spikes caused by electrostatic discharge events.
- The wiring for the CP2102 follows manufacturer recommendations, ensuring proper power and data transmission. Hierarchical labels are used to connect USB data signals between schematic sheets, enabling seamless communication between components. A voltage ladder, consisting of a 22.1kΩ and a 47.5kΩ resistor, helps regulate the voltage level for the VBus signal. Additionally, bypass capacitors are placed near the CP2102 to stabilize power delivery.
- LEDs are integrated into the circuit to indicate serial data transmission and reception. A blue LED for TX and a red LED for RX provide visual feedback of data activity. The circuit is enclosed within a labeled box for clarity, ensuring easy identification during later design stages.
- Source: Lecture 21-210 - USB-UART Bridge

#### Configuring the ESP32 Microcontroller
- The ESP32 is at the core of the schematic, responsible for processing and communication tasks. Proper wiring of its pins is critical for stable operation. A 50Ω resistor is placed on the I06 pin for signal conditioning. Multiple net labels are assigned to key pins, ensuring correct connections for SPI, I²C, and general-purpose input/output (GPIO) functions.
- The ESP32’s power supply requires careful management. It is connected to a stable 3.3V source, with bypass capacitors (10µF and 0.1µF) placed nearby for noise reduction. The enable (EN) and boot pins are labeled appropriately for future connections. Each connection follows the manufacturer’s data sheet guidelines to ensure compliance with recommended design practices.
- Source: Lecture 22-220 - ESP32

#### Implementing the SD Card Module
- The SD card module is an SPI-based device that communicates with the ESP32. The MOSI, MISO, clock, and chip select (CS) lines are wired according to SPI standards. A 10kΩ pull-up resistor is placed on the chip select line to ensure reliable communication.
- Signal integrity is managed with a 50Ω series resistor on the MISO line. Grounding considerations are taken into account, ensuring that all necessary pins are connected to the ground plane. The power input of the SD card module is connected to 3.3V, with two capacitors (10µF and 0.1µF) placed nearby to stabilize the voltage supply.
- Since the SD card can operate in either SPI or 4-bit SD mode, the unused data lines (DAT1 and DAT2) are grounded to avoid floating signals. The entire module is enclosed in a labeled box for clarity, visually distinguishing it from other parts of the schematic.
- Source: Lecture 23-230 - SD Card Module

#### Adding Flash Memory to the Circuit
- Flash memory is another SPI-based component that provides non-volatile storage for the ESP32. The SPI lines (MOSI, MISO, clock, and chip select) are connected to the ESP32 in the same manner as the SD card module.
- A 50Ω series resistor is added to the data line to improve signal integrity. The chip select line is pulled high using a resistor to prevent accidental selection when the SPI bus is in use. The hold pin and write-protect pin are tied to 3.3V to keep the memory module active and writable at all times.
- A bypass capacitor (0.1µF) is placed near the flash memory chip to stabilize the power supply. The entire circuit is enclosed within a labeled box, simplifying future modifications or troubleshooting.
- Source: Lecture 24-240 - Flash Memory

#### Expanding the Schematic with Sensor and User Interface Pages
- To maintain clarity in the design, two new hierarchical schematic pages are created: one for sensors and another for user interface components. These pages will contain additional circuitry while keeping the main schematic well-organized.
- The sensor page will include connections for environmental sensors, such as temperature and light sensors, while the user interface page will handle buttons, displays, and other interactive components. Each new page is given a distinct color in KiCad to improve visibility. The schematic legend is updated to reflect the changes, ensuring that each page is properly labeled for easy reference.

#### Adding Test Points for Debugging
- Test points are added to the schematic to facilitate troubleshooting and validation of the PCB. These small pads on the board allow engineers to connect measurement instruments, such as oscilloscopes or multimeters, to key signals without interfering with the circuit’s operation.
- Five test points are placed for monitoring the SPI bus: MOSI, MISO, clock, and the two chip select signals (for the SD card and flash memory). Each test point is labeled accordingly to ensure easy identification during testing.
- Minor adjustments are made to the component placement to improve accessibility. Capacitors are repositioned to be closer to the voltage supply pins for better performance. The final schematic is saved and reviewed before proceeding to the next design phase.
- Source: Lecture 26-260 - Add Test Points
