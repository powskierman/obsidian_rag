- The unit circle definition allows us to extend the domain of sine and cosine to $\mathbb R$.
- [[Angle]]s in the plane are positive when measured anticlockwise, and negative when measured clockwise. 
- To simplify computations, we fit an [[angle]]'s [[triangle]] into a circle with radius _r_ = 1. 
	- A circle with a radius of 1 is called "the unit circle". 
	- Once the hypotenuse has a fixed length of _r_ = 1, then the values of the trig ratios will depend only on _x_ and _y_, since multiplying or dividing by _r_ = 1 won't change anything. 
- #### Unit Circle
-  ![[Unit Circle Relationships.png|500]]
	- [More elaborate diagram of unit circle](Circle-trig6.svg)
	-  $x^2+y^2=1$
	- in the unit circle, for any angle θ: 
		- $\cos(\theta)=x$
		- $\sin(θ) = y$
		- $\tan(θ) = {y \over x}= {\sin(\theta)\over \cos(\theta)}$
	- When the angle is 0
		-  $\sin(0) = 0$
		- $\cos(0)=1$
		- $\tan(0) = 0$ 

-  $\sin(\theta)$ is the real number obtained by moving directly across to the vertical $y$ axis
- $cos(\theta)$ is the $\mathbb R$ obtained by moving down to the $x$ axis
- Points on the unit circle with angles θ equivalent to 0, $\pi \over 2$, $\pi$, $3\pi \over 2$ fall on the axes at the points (1, 0), (0, 1), (−1, 0), (0, −1) respectively.
- Memorize the unit circle using the **[[ASAP]]** mnemonic:
	- **All** - You need to memorize all of the first quadrant [[radians]].
	- **Subtract** - To get the numerator of each radian in the second quadrant, you subtract 1 from the denominator for the corresponding first quadrant angle.
	- **Add** - To get the numerator of each radian in the third quadrant, you add 1 to the denominator for the corresponding first quadrant angle.
	- **Prime numbers** - Each radian in the fourth quadrant starts with a prime number.

- ### Process for determining the sine/cosine of $\LARGE \theta$ :
 - Starting from (1,0), move along the unit circle in the counterclockwise direction until the angle that is formed between your position, the origin, and the positive x-axis is equal to $\large \theta$.
 - sin⁡(θ) is equal to the y-coordinate of your point, and cos⁡(θ) is equal to the x-coordinate.
 - If [[radians]] is 4, angle is multiple of $45^\circ$ ex: ${3 \pi}\over 4$

 - ### Solve trig angles of special values
  - ### Strategy
   - Draw the desired angle on the unit circle and find a corresponding right [[triangle]].
   - Solve the right [[triangle]] using the known trigonometric values of the [[Special Triangles]], or their complementary angles.
   - A complimentary angle is $90^\circ-\theta$
	- #### Special trig values
		- Trigonometric values for 0:
			- $\sin⁡(0)=0$
			- $\cos⁡(0)=1$
		- Trigonometric values for $\pi \over 6$:
			- $\sin⁡({\pi \over 6})={1 \over 2}$
			- $\cos⁡({\pi \over 6})={\sqrt 3 \over 2}$
		- Trigonometric values for $\pi \over 4$:
			- $\sin⁡({\pi \over 4})={\sqrt 2 \over 2}$
			- $\cos⁡({\pi \over 4})={\sqrt 2 \over 2}$
		- Trigonometric values for $\pi \over 3$:
			- $\sin⁡({\pi \over 3})={\sqrt 3 \over 2}$
			- $\cos⁡({\pi \over 3})={1 \over 2}$
		- 
- ### How to Calculate the Sine of Special Angles in Degrees
	- $\sin(a + b) = (\sin a \times\cos b)+\cos(a) \times \sin(b)$
	- $\sin(a - b) = (\sin a \times\cos b)-\cos(a) \times \sin(b)$
