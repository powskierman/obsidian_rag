### Notes
##### Initial Treatment Options
- Initial Therapy
	- Dose-Adjusted EPOCH-R (DA-EPOCH-R):
		- DA-EPOCH-R is considered the preferred frontline treatment for most DHL patients due to its efficacy in achieving complete remission (CR) rates of up to 74% in prospective trials.
		- This regimen has shown durable event-free survival in approximately 71% of paties at 4 years1.
		-<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;"> Dr. Slaby discounted this option because of the toll this treatment would take on my body.</mark>
	- R-CHOP:
		- While historically used, R-CHOP has demonstrated inferior outcomes compared to more intensive regimens in retrospective studies. However, some patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) morphology may achieve progression-free survival with R-CHOP.
		- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">This is the chosen treatment option.</mark>
	- R-CODOX-M/R-IVAC:
		- This highly intensive regimen has shown favorable outcomes but is typically reserved for younger, fit patients due to its toxicity profile.
	- CNS Prophylaxis:
		- Intrathecal methotrexate is often included in DA-EPOCH-R protocols to address the risk of central nervous system (CNS) involvement.
		- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Dr. Slaby considers the risk/reward ratio unfavorable.</mark>
- Radiation Therapy:
	- For limited-stage disease, consolidative involved-site <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">radiation therapy may be beneficial due to the chemotherapy-resistant nature of DHL.<mark>
##### Relapsed/Refractory Disease
- CAR T-Cell Therapy:
	- Anti-CD19 CAR T-cell therapies, such as axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel) and lisocabtagene maraleucel (liso-cel), have emerged as <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">standard second-line options for relapsed/refractory DHL.</mark>
	- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Both therapies demonstrated significant improvements in progression-free survival compared to traditional salvage chemotherapy and autologous stem cell transplant (#ASCT)<mark>.
- Bispecific Antibodies:
	- Agents like glofitamab and epcoritamab target CD20/CD3 have shown promise in heavily pretreated patients, achieving complete response rates of up to 40%.
##### Other Therapies:
- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">Additional options include antibody-drug conjugates such as polatuzumab vedotin or loncastuximab tesirine, as well as selinexor, a selective inhibitor of nuclear export.</mark>
- Tafasitamab-lenalidomide is less commonly used due to disappointing real-world outcomes in high-risk patients.
##### Late Relapse Management:
- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">For patients with late relapse, high-dose chemotherapy followed by #ASCT remains the standard for transplant-eligible individuals.</mark> Non-transplant eligible patients may benefit from liso-cel based on recent trials.
##### Key Considerations
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Achieving complete remission with frontline therapy is the most critical prognostic factor.</mark>
- Treatment failure typically occurs within the first year; thus, <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">early intervention with CAR T-cell therapy or bispecific antibodies is essential for relapsed cases.</mark>
- The choice of therapy should consider my age, fitness, and disease stage.
##### In summary 
- While DA-EPOCH-R remains the cornerstone of initial treatment for DHL, advances such as CAR T-cell therapy and bispecific antibodies are reshaping management strategies for relapsed/refractory disease.
