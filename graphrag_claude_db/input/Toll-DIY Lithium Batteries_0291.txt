## Highlights
Most li-ion cells have a nominal voltage of between 3.6 V to 3.7 V and are usually rated for a discharge-charge voltage range of 2.5 V - 4.2 V. — location: [263](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B06XRKD15B&location=263) ^ref-29748

---
Discharging below 2.5 V will cause irreparable damage to the cell, resulting in the cell not holding its rated capacity or sustaining its rated discharge current. — location: [267](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B06XRKD15B&location=267) ^ref-43712

---
The maximum discharge rating tells you the maximum load, which is to say the maximum current, that can be drawn from the cell. There are actually two common discharge ratings, the “maximum continuous discharge current” and the “maximum peak discharge current”. The maximum continuous discharge current is the better figure to use when making comparisons between cells. — location: [569](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B06XRKD15B&location=569) ^ref-57028

---
The C rate of a battery cell is a measurement of the rate that the battery cell can be discharged or charged in relation to the cell’s capacity. — location: [585](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B06XRKD15B&location=585) ^ref-11905

---
It is recommended not to charge most lithium cells at more than 0.5 C, and charging closer to 0.2 C is much better for the cell’s health. — location: [610](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B06XRKD15B&location=610) ^ref-56086

---
I like to use 8 mm wide strips of 0.15 mm thickness, and will often use multiple strips welded on top of each other when a higher current battery is required. — location: [1215](kindle://book?action=open&asin=B06XRKD15B&location=1215) ^ref-27526
