### Notes
- - [[constant test]]
- The [[chain rule]] allows us to find derivatives of [[composition]]s
- [[quotient rule]] 
-  The [[power rule]]
- A [[derivative]] is [[additive]]:
	- $f(x)=u(x)+v(x)=>f'(x)=u'(x)+v'(x)$
- [[constant]]s come out the front:
	- $f(x)=kg(x)=>f'(x)=kg'(x)$
- [[derivative]] of [[polynomials]]
	- [[differenciation|differentiate]] each power of $x$
	- multiply through by [[constant|constants]]
	- add up the terms
	- example: $f(x)=x^4-2x^3+5x^2-3x+8$
		- $f'(x)=4x^3-2(3x^2)+5(2x)-3(1)+0$=$f'(x)= 4x^3-6x^2+10x-3$
		- $f''(x)=4(3x^2)-6(2x)+10$=$12x^2-12x+10$
		- $f'''(x)=12(2x)-12=24x$
		- $f''''(x)=24$
		- $f'''''(x)=0$
			- [[differenciation|differentiating]] a quadratic 3 times makes it disappear
- $y=e^x$
	- $f(x)=e^x=>f'(x)=e^x$
		- indestructable property
	- The [[slope]] of the [[tangent line]] at $y$ intercept is 1
	- $\lim\limits_{h \to 0}{e^h-1\over h}=1$
	- ![[Ln.png]]
- $f(x)=\sin x => f'(x)=\cos x$
- $f(x)=\cos x => f'(x)=-\sin x$
	- Also indestructible:
		- $f(x)=\sin x => f'(x)=\cos x=>f''(x)=-\sin x => f'''(x)=-\cos x=>f''''(x)=\sin x$
- [[simple harmonic motion]]
-   [[derivative]] of [[square root]] of $x$
	- $y=x^2$ so $x=\sqrt y$
		- $\frac {dy}{dx}=2x$ = $2\sqrt y$
		- ${\frac {d}{dy}(\sqrt y)}$=$1 \over {2 \sqrt y}$
		- ${\frac {d}{dx}(\sqrt x)}$=$1 \over {2 \sqrt x}$ so derivative of square root of $x$ is $1 \over {2 \sqrt x}$
	- Using fractional power notation:
		-  ${\frac {d}{dx}(x^{1\over 2})}$=$1 \over {2 x^{1 \over 2}}$ = ${1 \over 2} x{^{-{1 \over 2}}}$ so:
			- ${\frac {d}{dx}(x^{1\over 2})}$= ${1 \over 2} x{^{-{1 \over 2}}}$
		- Which is consistent with:
			-  ${\frac {d}{dx}(x^n)}$= $nx{^{n-1}}$
	- [[derivative]] of cube root of $x$
		- ${\frac {d}{dx}(x^{1\over 3})}$= ${1 \over 3} x{^{-{2 \over 3}}}$
- [[derivative]] of $\ln(x)$ is reciprocal of $x$
	- ${d \over dx}\ln(x)={1\over x}$
