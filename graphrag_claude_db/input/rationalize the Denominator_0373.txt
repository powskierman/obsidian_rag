- To rationalize is to move the root from the bottom of a fraction (denominator) to the top of the fraction (numerator). This way, the denominator becomes rational.
- **Step 1:** Multiply both the  and  by a suitable  that will remove the radicals in the denominator.
-  **Step 2:** We need to make sure that all the surds in the given fraction are in their simplified form.
-  **Step 3:** If needed, we can simplify the fraction further.
- Ex: If $a ∈ Z$, $b ∈ N$ and $a ± b \ne 0$ then
	- $1 \over(a ∓ √b)$ = ($1 \over(a ∓ √b)$)($(a ∓ √b) \over(a ∓ √b)$)=$(a ∓ √b) \over (a^2 - b)$.
